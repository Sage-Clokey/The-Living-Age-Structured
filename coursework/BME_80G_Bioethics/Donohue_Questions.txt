Questions for Dr. Donohue — BME 80G Speaker Seminar

1. In your talk description, you note that post-war geneticists like Dobzhansky argued eugenics could now be implemented "scientifically" and "ethically." How did they reconcile this claim with the fact that the underlying logic — ranking human variation as superior or inferior — remained unchanged from the pre-war eugenics movement? Was the shift genuinely scientific, or was it a rebranding of the same hierarchical thinking?

2. You trace the continuity of eugenic reasoning from the late 1950s through the beginnings of the Human Genome Project. Given that modern technologies like IVF embryo screening and polygenic risk scoring now allow individuals to make selection decisions that were once imposed by the state, do you see this as a meaningful ethical distinction — or does the same machine-like logic of pruning human variation persist, just relocated from government programs to consumer choice?
