# EP02 — Who Owns Your Life?

**Series:** True Republic
**Type:** Street / Campus Interview
**Core Question:** "Who owns your life?"

---

## The Seed Being Planted

Self-ownership is the foundation of all libertarian and voluntaryist ethics. Most people
intuitively believe they own themselves — but they've never examined whether their relationship
with the state is consistent with that belief.

If you own yourself, no one has the right to your labor, your body, or your choices without consent.
If someone else owns you, that is the definition of slavery — even if it's called taxation, conscription,
or licensing.

---

## Interview Arc

### Opening Question
"Who owns your life?"

### Follow-Up Questions
- "So you own yourself?"
- "Does that mean you own the product of your labor?"
- "If someone takes a portion of your paycheck without asking — what do we call that?"
- "What if a large group votes to take it — does the vote change the nature of the act?"
- "If you own your body, should you be allowed to put whatever you want into it?"
- "Who gets to override the owner of a life — and by what right?"

### The Contradiction That Often Emerges
People will affirm self-ownership and then defend government programs that require
overriding it. The moment of discovery: either you own yourself or you don't.
There's no partial ownership that isn't just a different word for controlled.

---

## Cold Open Script (Narrator)
> "There are two possible answers to this question.
> Either you own your life — or someone else does.
> Everything in politics follows from which answer you give."

---

## Closing Narration
The shaken jar makes us argue about *who* should own us. The living question is
whether any external party has a legitimate claim on a life they didn't create,
sustain, or consent to serve.
