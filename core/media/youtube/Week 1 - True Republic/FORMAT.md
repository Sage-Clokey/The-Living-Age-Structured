# True Republic — Video Format

## Purpose
Gently guide people toward voluntaryism and decentralized thinking through real conversation.
Not debate. Not argument. Seed planting.

The goal is to ask the most dangerous question available:
"Who is shaking the jar?"

---

## The Core Parable (From The Sagent Creed)

Picture a glass jar. Inside it live one hundred red ants and one hundred black ants.
Someone picks up the jar and shakes it violently. When the shaking stops, the red ants
look around and see black ants. The black ants look around and see red ants.
Each group arrives at the same conclusion: they did this. They are the enemy. The fighting begins.

The shaker watches from outside. Undisturbed.

This is the machinery of manufactured division. The genius of it is that it becomes
self-sustaining — each act of aggression confirms the original assumption. Real wounds
make real enemies. And real enemies make the shaker's work easy, and eventually unnecessary.

The True Republic series asks: what happens when an ant stops fighting and asks —
"Who picked up the jar?"

---

## The Core Method
Socratic questioning — ask a simple question, let them reason, ask a deeper question,
let the contradiction appear naturally. The viewer watches someone discover the idea themselves.

To fight the hand that shakes the jar using the same methods the shaker uses does not work.
Evil cannot be defeated by those who adopt its methods.
The red ants who counter-manipulate with their own grievance narratives do not escape the jar —
they become accomplices in the shaking.

Sovereignty of mind is the only answer. Each person thinking clearly represents a node of
resistance to the shaking.

## Episode Types

### Type A — Street Interview
- Approach strangers in public spaces (parks, campuses, markets)
- Ask one central philosophical question
- Let them talk. Follow their reasoning.
- Duration: 5–10 minutes

### Type B — Conference / Event Interview
- Target events: FreedomFest, SynBioBeta, liberty conferences, philosophy meetups
- More informed guests, deeper conversation
- Duration: 10–20 minutes

### Type C — Campus Conversation
- Engage students on university campuses
- Philosophy, political science, biology students make great guests
- Duration: 5–15 minutes

---

## Episode Structure

1. **Cold open** (0:00–0:20)
   - Show the question on screen before the interview begins
   - No introduction yet — hook first

2. **The Question** (0:20–1:00)
   - Ask the opening question simply and calmly
   - Example: "Do you think government authority is real?"

3. **Let Them Reason** (1:00–3:00)
   - Stay quiet. Let them answer fully.
   - Do not argue. Nod. Ask: "Why do you think that?"

4. **Deepen the Question** (3:00–5:00)
   - Follow their logic to its next step
   - Example: "So if someone didn't consent to be governed, is the authority still real?"

5. **The Contradiction Appears** (5:00–7:00)
   - The guest finds a tension in their own reasoning
   - You don't point it out — they discover it
   - This is the shaken jar moment: they are starting to ask who is holding the jar

6. **Closing Narration** (7:00–8:00)
   - Short narration over b-roll summarizing the seed planted
   - Connect to the shaken jar parable: directed anger flows horizontally toward fellow
     sufferers rather than upward toward the source
   - No lecture. No conclusion forced on the viewer.

## Example Opening Questions

- "Is government authority real, or is it just an agreement?"
- "Who owns your life?"
- "Would society collapse without rulers?"
- "What is the difference between law and morality?"
- "If you didn't consent to be governed, are you still obligated to obey?"
- "What makes one person's authority over another legitimate?"
- "Is taxation voluntary?"
- "What is a True Republic?"
- "Who benefits from people fighting each other over politics?"
- "Is there a difference between law and morality — and which one actually governs your life?"

---

## Tone
- Curious, not confrontational
- Warm, not combative
- Patient, not pushy
- Think: a philosopher on a park bench, not a debater on a stage
- "The wisdom that might actually save the ants cannot be handed down from above.
   It can only emerge when individuals think freely and honestly." — The Sagent Creed

## Visual Style
- Handheld or stabilized camera
- Natural light
- Real environments (parks, streets, campuses)
- Minimal editing — keep it authentic
- Soft music underneath during narration sections only

## Equipment Needed
- Phone or camera on a small tripod or gimbal
- Lavalier mic clipped to guest (or directional mic)
- Natural setting

## Upload Cadence
Every 4 weeks (Week 1 of each month)
