# There Is No Commons — There Are Property Rights and Trade

**Sage Clokey**
**The Living Age — Distributed Knowledge Series**

---

## The Problem with the Commons Framing

The "tragedy of the commons" and Ostrom's "governing the commons" both start from the same assumption: that shared resources exist and need collective management. The only disagreement is whether the state or the community manages better. Ostrom proved communities do it better. But the deeper question is whether the framing itself is wrong.

There is no commons. There are sovereign agents with property rights who trade voluntarily. The commons framing smuggles in the assumption that resources need collective management — which is one step away from saying they need a manager. And that's the state again.

---

## The Cell Has Property Rights

The cell is not sharing a commons. It owns its internal state. It owns its membrane. It owns its receptors. It owns its genome, its organelles, its metabolic machinery. The membrane is the property line — it defines what is inside and what is outside, what belongs to this agent and what doesn't.

Every exchange across that boundary is a bilateral trade between property-holding agents:

- **Gap junctions** — two cells build the channel together. Either can refuse. Both must participate. It is a trade agreement, not access to a shared pool.
- **Extracellular vesicles** — the sender packages cargo and releases it. The receiver binds it through surface receptors, internalizes it, and translates the contents. The receiver has a customs agency. Nothing enters without consent (Valadi et al., 2007).
- **Tunneling nanotubes** — both cells extend membrane protrusions that fuse. Active transport machinery moves cargo along the tube. Either cell can refuse to participate (Rustom et al., 2004).
- **Microchimerism** — the mother's immune system actively tolerates the foreign fetal cells. If it rejected them, they would be destroyed. Persistence requires ongoing consent (Bianchi et al., 1996).

None of this is a commons. It is trade between sovereign agents across clearly defined property lines.

---

## Cancer Is Not a Tragedy of the Commons

Cancer is not a governance failure in a shared pool. Cancer is a cell that violates the property rights of its neighbors.

It ignores their growth factor signals — refusing to hear "stop." It recruits blood supply through angiogenesis — diverting resources that other cells were using. It invades surrounding tissue — crossing property lines without consent. It suppresses the immune cells that would normally remove it — silencing the enforcement mechanism.

This is not overuse of a shared resource. This is aggression. It is a cell that stopped trading and started taking. It stopped reading price signals and started imposing its will.

The difference matters. If cancer is a tragedy of the commons, the solution is better governance — more rules, more monitoring, more management. If cancer is a violation of property rights, the solution is restoring the sovereignty of the agents and the enforcement of boundaries. The immune system does exactly this — it doesn't manage a commons. It identifies and removes agents that violate the voluntary order.

---

## Why This Distinction Matters

The commons framing leads to central planning. If the tissue is a shared resource, someone needs to manage it. If the genome is a shared inheritance, someone needs to regulate it. If health is a public good, someone needs to mandate it.

The property rights framing leads to freedom. If each cell is a sovereign agent, the role of the system is to protect property lines and enforce consent — not to manage resource allocation from above. The immune system doesn't plan the economy of the body. It protects the conditions under which cells can trade freely and removes agents that cheat, coerce, or monopolize.

The same distinction applies at every scale:
- The cell owns its genome. Trade is voluntary. Cancer is aggression.
- The individual owns their body. Trade is voluntary. The state is aggression.
- The community owns its local knowledge. Trade is voluntary. Federal mandates are aggression.

There is no commons. There are free agents, clear boundaries, and voluntary exchange. That is how life works at every scale the data tests.
