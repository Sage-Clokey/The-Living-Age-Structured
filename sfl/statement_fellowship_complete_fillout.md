### 05. What is the riskiest assumption underneath your project?

**The assumption [one sentence]:**
That STEM students who intuitively understand emergent order from their training will engage with the political-philosophical implications when presented through biology rather than ideology.

**How you plan to test it [150 words]:**
Three "Is Nature Libertarian?" events in 90 days. Three different campus types — research university, state school, community college. Three things measured: (1) who shows up — sign-in sheets track major and department, so I can see whether non-political, non-libertarian STEM students attend; (2) who stays connected — how many sign up for follow-up content or join the network after; (3) what they say — the sign-in sheet ends with one question: "What question are you leaving with?" That tells me whether the biology-to-philosophy bridge landed or bounced. The event template, sign-in system, and follow-up pipeline are already built. If biology-framed events draw twice the non-political-student attendance of a standard SFL event at the same campus, the assumption holds.

**What you would do if the assumption proved wrong [100 words]:**
If the biology lands but the philosophical bridge does not, I reshape the project toward pure science communication. "Cancer Is Central Planning" and "The Open Source Economy of the Cell" work as science education whether or not the audience draws the liberty conclusion. The event format shifts from Socratic discussion to science talk. The audience still grows. The seed is still planted. The explicit bridge becomes implicit. I do not kill the project — I let the content stand on its own and trust the pattern to do the work over time. Seeds, not pamphlets.
