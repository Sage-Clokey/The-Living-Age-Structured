# Email to Dr. Bernick — Evolution Essay

**Subject:** A draft I'd value your feedback on — evolution as spontaneous order

---

Dr. Bernick,

I've been writing during the gap year, and one piece has turned into something I think is worth sharing with you. It's called "Evolution Is Spontaneous Order" — the argument that biological evolution and economic spontaneous order are not analogous but structurally identical. Distributed agents reading local signals, producing emergent order without central planning. Same process at every scale.

The essay draws on Shapiro's natural genetic engineering, Monroe et al.'s mutation bias work, Woese and Koonin on horizontal gene transfer, Noë and Hammerstein's biological markets, and Kauffman's self-organization — and maps them against the Austrian economists' framework for how complex systems coordinate without a central planner.

A few sections I'd especially appreciate your perspective on:

- **Codon optimization and STEALTH.** I use your work as the key example of designing *with* the living system's grammar rather than overwriting it. Naive codon optimization is central planning applied to translation — maximizing one metric while destroying the distributed information in codon choice. STEALTH is the alternative: respecting the host's codon pair biases and translational rhythms. I want to make sure I'm representing the logic accurately.

- **HGT as trade.** This extends the conversations we've had. I'm framing horizontal gene transfer as a distributed exchange network that follows trade logic — the "price" of acquisition is metabolic cost, the "profit" is fitness gain, and organisms in more connected environments show higher transfer rates the way firms in larger markets show more trade. This is the theoretical foundation for the first research direction in my PhD proposal.

- **Cancer as the Cantillon effect.** The Warburg effect mapped onto monetary economics — cancer cells with corrupted price signals (mTOR, AMPK, EGFR mutations) consume glucose at 18x lower efficiency and starve neighboring tissue, the way newly printed money enriches those closest to the source. I think this framing generates testable predictions using TCGA and FDG-PET data.

The essay is about 6,000 words with 30+ references. I've attached it as a markdown file. If you have time to read it, I'd welcome any pushback — especially where the biology outpaces what the data currently supports.

This is the theoretical framework behind the three research directions I'm building my PBSE application around. Your feedback would sharpen it considerably.

Sage
