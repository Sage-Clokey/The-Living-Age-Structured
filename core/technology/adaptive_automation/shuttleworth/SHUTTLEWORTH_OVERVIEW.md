# Shuttleworth Foundation — Overview & Strategy

## Who They Are

Founded by **Mark Shuttleworth** — South African entrepreneur who built and sold a
security company to VeriSign for $575M, then used that money to fund Canonical,
the company behind Ubuntu Linux. In 2002 he paid Russia $20M to fly to the ISS
as a self-funded astronaut — the second person ever to do so privately.

The foundation is his attempt to answer: *what's the highest-leverage thing money
can do?* His answer: find exceptional people with unconventional ideas the mainstream
won't fund, give them freedom and resources, and get out of the way.

---

## What Makes It Different

Most foundations fund projects. Shuttleworth funds **people**.

The theory: the person *is* the project. The vision, the worldview, the capacity
to execute and adapt are what determine outcomes — not the specific deliverable
in a grant proposal.

Application format: **three short videos** speaking directly to camera.
No 15-page technical document. No budget justification at application stage.

---

## Financial Structure

- **Fellowship stipend:** funds living expenses and basic operating costs for a year
- **Project funding:** additional funds specifically for the work
- **10:1 personal match:** if you invest your own money, Shuttleworth matches it 10:1
- **Duration:** one year, renewable up to three years total
- **Conditions:** full-time on the project, open-source the work, join the fellow community

No equity. No IP claim. No board seat.

## Past Fellows (The Pattern)

**Marcin Jakubowski** — Open Source Ecology. Open-source blueprints for 50
industrial machines (tractors, CNC mills, brick presses). Thesis: civilization-scale
tools should belong to everyone.

**Adam Hyde** — Open publishing infrastructure and book sprint methodology.
Open tools for knowledge creation.

**Peter Bloom** — Community-owned internet infrastructure. Decentralized connectivity.

**The pattern:** people who believe a critical piece of infrastructure or knowledge
is locked behind walls it shouldn't be behind — and are building the open version.

## Why The Living Age Fits

Mark Shuttleworth built Ubuntu because the operating system — the foundation
everything runs on — should not be owned by Microsoft. Access to computational
infrastructure is a prerequisite for human agency.

**The living architecture argument is structurally identical:**

The design tools for biological systems should not be owned by Ginkgo Bioworks
or a defense contractor. The grammar of life — the protein language, the regulatory
logic, the developmental programs — should be open infrastructure.

That argument will resonate at a deep level because it's the same argument
Shuttleworth made about software, now applied to biology.

## What's Already Working in Our Favor

- Philosophy is unusually coherent — the worldview is documented across multiple
  texts before anyone was watching
- Existing codebase is evidence of motion — not just an idea, something built
- UCSC bioinformatics background gives technical credibility
- Open-source strategy is genuine — compatibility engine and GRN database make
  sense as commons infrastructure, not just performance

## What Would Strengthen the Application

- One working Phase 1 demo (English → protein → DNA, end-to-end)
- One person using it who isn't you (iGEM team, academic collaborator)
- A crisp answer to "why must this be open?" — specifically: if Ginkgo owns the
  protein design tools for living materials, what happens to small labs, independent
  researchers, architects without biotech partnerships?

Target: **August window** — achievable with Phase 1 demo complete.
