# Shuttleworth Foundation Fellowship — Application

**Amount:** ~1 year salary + expenses + 10:1 match on personal investment (up to 3 years renewable)
**Equity/IP:** None. No IP claims. Non-dilutive. Must open-source your work.
**Deadline:** May 1, 2026 (for September 2026 start) | November 1 (for March start)
**Time to apply:** ~4-5 hours (written application + 3 short videos)
**Apply here:** https://www.shuttleworthfoundation.org/apply/
**Program page:** https://www.shuttleworthfoundation.org/

---

## How It Works

- The foundation funds **people**, not projects
- Fellows receive a salary + expense budget for 1 year (renewable up to 3x)
- **10:1 match**: For every $1 you invest of your own money, they invest $10
- **Open requirement**: All work must be open (open source, open data, open content)
- Mark Shuttleworth (founder of Ubuntu/Canonical) reviews applications personally
- Fellows join a community of ~15-20 active fellows working on open tools worldwide

---

## What They Fund

Past fellows have worked on:
- Open textbooks (Siyavula)
- Open hardware (TAPR, Safecast)
- Open science tools
- Open education platforms
- Open data infrastructure

The common thread: **open tools that shift power toward individuals and communities.**

#### What are you working on?

I must finish what my grandfather started. My grandfather was Art Clokey, the
creator of Gumby — a green figure made of clay who bent without breaking, who
showed millions that dead matter could be made to live through imagination.
I am carrying that forward with biology instead of clay.

LivingCAD — the first organism-scale biodesign platform that translates plain
English into synthesizable DNA sequences for structural organisms. The core
infrastructure — compatibility engine, gene regulatory network database, and
the bridge layer connecting English to protein generation — will be fully
open source.

The near-term product: open design tools for synthetic biologists. The
long-term vision: living houses grown from engineered fungi. The philosophy:
life is not a block you carve into shape; it is a spiral you cultivate.

The creation of the Living Age requires media to explain why and technology
to show how. I'm building both — the open philosophical framework and the
open design platform. One without the other doesn't work.

The design tools for biological systems are being built by a small number of
well-funded companies as proprietary platforms. If these tools remain closed,
the ability to design living materials will be concentrated in whoever can
afford platform access. LivingCAD's open core ensures any iGEM team, any
small lab, any architect experimenting with mycelium, any researcher in any
country gets the same design tools as a team at MIT.

#### What already exists?

Working code (not a pitch deck):
- Genome retrieval system querying 220+ genomes from UCSC and NCBI
- Codon optimization engine with validated RSCU tables for 5 chassis organisms
- Regulatory element compatibility analysis (24 characterized parts)
- Pathway conflict detection (12 metabolic/signaling profiles)
- Adaptive assembly engine
- Claude API integration translating English → structured biological specs

#### Why does openness matter for this work?

The grammar of life should not be owned. DNA is a language that evolved over
4 billion years. The tools to speak it back — to design organisms — must be
public infrastructure. If biological design becomes proprietary, only those
who can afford platform licenses will shape the living future. Open biological
design tools are as fundamental as open-source operating systems were in the
1990s.

Mark Shuttleworth demonstrated this with Ubuntu: the operating system as
public infrastructure. LivingCAD applies the same logic to the operating
system of life itself.
