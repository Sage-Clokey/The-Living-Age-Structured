A Manifesto for the Living Age

There comes a time when every generation must decide whether it will merely preserve the past or create the future. We inherit stories, technologies, traditions, and institutions, but we are not called simply to maintain them unchanged. We are called to breathe life into them, refine them, and carry them forward. That time has come.

The Inheritance

It began with those who came before me.

My grandfather, Art Clokey, created Gumby from clay. My father, Joe Clokey, devoted his life to carrying that creative vision forward. Around them grew a collection of seemingly different works, yet I see them as expressions of a common purpose.

There are certain callings that have run through my family, each expressing a different part of what it means to live and create:

Film — Art Clokey. Through Gumby, he showed that media could be created through real craftsmanship: clay, sets, practical effects, and imagination made tangible.

Agriculture — Joe Clokey. Through San Luis Video Publishing, he worked to preserve and spread practical knowledge about agriculture and stewardship of the land.

Education & Music — Joseph Walden Clokey. As a professor of classical music, he devoted his life to teaching, cultivating excellence, and passing culture from one generation to the next.

Religion — Joseph Parkander. As a Lutheran pastor at Augustana, he dedicated his life to conscience, faith, and the relationship between humanity and God.

These are all fields that have increasingly been overtaken by the machine. Yet each of these men sought to practice them in a living, human way—the way of the spiral rather than the block.

Together they point toward a single vision.

The Fall and the Pattern

There was a time when these ideas still existed together. Then they became scattered. Some works changed ownership. Some lost their original direction. Some simply faded from public attention. What had once been a unified creative vision became fragmented.

The pieces remained. The unity was lost.

This fragmentation follows a recurring pattern. A living creation is born. It flourishes. It enters systems that preserve it. Over time those systems forget the purpose for which it was created. The form survives. The spirit becomes hidden.

Then comes the call to restoration.

The Return

It is time for Clokey Productions to rise again from the ashes—renewed for a new age.

Its purpose is not simply to make entertainment. Its purpose is to create living media.

Clokey Productions exists to create media grounded in reality.

• • •

Real voices.

Real instruments.

Real performers.

Real craftsmanship.

Real practical effects.

• • •

Technology is not the enemy. Technology is a tool. It should extend reality, not replace it.

Digital sound can help composers experiment. Artificial intelligence can help organize ideas. Computers can help artists prototype worlds that have never existed before. But the final work should be brought back into the physical world whenever possible. The purpose of technology is to serve human creativity, not substitute for it.

This is what Clokey Productions stands for: practical media made by real people.

Media and Technology

Clokey Productions is the media wing of the Living Age.

The Living Age has two complementary callings. One is media, which asks why. The other is technology, which answers how. They strengthen one another in a continual cycle.

Media inspires people to imagine a future that does not yet exist. Technology gives humanity the tools to build it. The new technologies then become the inspiration for new stories, and those stories inspire another generation of creators and inventors.

Media and technology are not competitors. They are partners in creation.

Media to show why. Technology to show how.

Stories shape the imagination. The imagination shapes invention. Invention reshapes reality.

The future has never been changed primarily by politics. Politics usually follows culture rather than leading it. Culture is shaped by media. Media inspires technology. Technology transforms daily life. Daily life changes culture.

That is the true cycle that moves civilization.

The Mission

Clokey Productions exists to create media that reminds people what it means to be alive.

To celebrate practical craftsmanship.

To celebrate imagination.

To celebrate conscience.

To celebrate real music, real storytelling, real people, real creation.

The Living Age calls artists and technologists to work together. Technology expands what humanity can build. Media expands what humanity believes it can build. One gives the world new tools. The other gives the world new dreams.

Civilizations require both.

The Foundation

When you realize that the thing you are called to do may eventually cost you everything, it does not mean you should abandon it. It means you should begin with principle.

First comes separation. Then comes preparation. Only afterward comes the return. The foundation must be firm before the rise.

A person who has principles that cannot be purchased, no matter the price, is dangerous to anyone who depends upon buying loyalty instead of earning trust. When you see what someone refuses to sell, you begin to understand what they truly value.

That is the foundation of Clokey Productions.

The future belongs not merely to those who seek power,

but to those who create with wisdom, responsibility, and conscience.

Every invention first exists as a story.

Every civilization first exists as an idea.

Every transformation begins in the human mind.

That is the calling of the Living Age.

The Living Age does not seek to rule people's minds through propaganda. It seeks to inspire people through beauty, craftsmanship, wonder, and conscience. Together, media and technology help humanity imagine—and then build—a better world.
