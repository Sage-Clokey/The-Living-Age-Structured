## The North Star

Grow living houses.

Not houses made of biological material. Not houses decorated with plants.
Houses that **are** organisms — that grow from a seed, form themselves into
shelter, strengthen under load, repair their own damage, breathe with the seasons,
and return to the earth when their time is done.

This is not science fiction. Every mechanism required already exists in nature.
Mycelium already grows load-bearing structures. Coral already builds mineral
architecture from seawater. Planaria already regenerate entire bodies from fragments.
Arabidopsis already grows toward light. The sea urchin already builds a skeleton
from a single cell following a genetic blueprint.

What does not yet exist is the **design language** — the system that lets a human
speak intent and watch life write the answer.

This plan is the path from here to there.

## The Core Argument

The block-world gave civilization extraordinary things.
It also taught a single devastating lie: that humans are separate from nature.

Industrial design begins by killing: trees become lumber, soil becomes substrate,
living systems become raw material. Only once life is stripped away can the block
be imposed. The result is rigid, brittle, metabolically expensive, ecologically
destructive, and ultimately dead.

Living design begins with a different question:
*What does life already know how to do?*

A tree distributes stress through every branching joint without an engineer.
A fungal network moves nutrients with more efficiency than any supply chain.
A coral reef builds mineral complexity across kilometers without a blueprint.
A planarian regenerates its brain from a fragment of its tail.

These are not curiosities. They are proofs. Life has spent 3.8 billion years solving
the exact problems we are trying to solve: structure, strength, repair, growth,
environmental adaptation, resource efficiency.

The question is not whether life can build houses.
The question is: **can we learn to ask it to?**

## The Three Biological Problems

To grow a living house, three problems must be solved in sequence:

### Problem 1: Molecular Machines (Proteins)
What does each gene actually do? Can we design new proteins for functions
that don't exist in nature, or that exist in organisms we can't use directly?
→ Solved by the **Protein Layer** (ESM-3, Phase 1)

### Problem 2: Control (Regulation)
When and where does each gene turn on? A self-repairing house needs genes that
activate when damage occurs, not all the time. A load-bearing wall needs
to grow stronger when weight is applied. This requires regulatory circuits —
designed enhancers, promoters, and transcription factor networks.
→ Solved by the **Regulatory Layer** (Enformer + Nucleotide Transformer, Phase 2)

### Problem 3: Form (Morphogenesis)
How does a single seeded culture grow into a specific complex 3D shape?
This is the hardest problem. It requires a **developmental program** —
a gene regulatory network that gives cells positional identity, tells them
what to build based on where they are, and produces emergent geometry
from local rules.
→ Solved by the **Developmental Layer** (GRN design + morphogenesis engine, Phases 3-4)

## The Organisms (The Living Chassis)

### Primary Structural Organism: Ganoderma lucidum (Reishi Mycelium)

**Why Ganoderma:**
- Grows in any 3D shape — put it in a mold and it fills it
- Produces chitin-glucan matrix: 10× stronger than concrete per unit weight
- Already demonstrated at scale (Ecovative Design: fungal packaging, furniture)
- Ecologically restorative: sequesters carbon as it grows, decomposes naturally
- Native habitat: dead and living wood — it already builds in structures
- Hyphae regrow after damage — natural self-repair baseline
- Our codon tables and compatibility engine already support it

**What we design into it:**
- Enhanced cross-linking enzymes (laccases, peroxidases) for greater structural strength
- Mechanosensing circuit: grow stronger under sustained load
- Damage-response circuit: detect rupture → activate hyphal regrowth
- Directional growth circuits: phototropism, gravitropism, thigmotropism
- Mineral deposition: coral-derived galaxin for surface hardening
- Stress reporter: bioluminescence gated by mechanical load threshold

### Secondary Structural System: Bacterial Cellulose (Komagataeibacter)

**Why K. xylinus:**
- Produces pure cellulose sheets (pellicles) — flexible, strong, transparent
- Already used for wound dressings, biodegradable packaging
- Grows fast (mm/day), controllable by substrate chemistry
- Ideal for windows, membranes, flexible connectors between structural elements
- Our compatibility engine already has its codon tables

**What we design into it:**
- UV-filtering compounds (sunscreen proteins from coral zooxanthellae)
- Moisture-regulating aquaporins
- Improved tensile strength (spider silk peptide domains in cellulose matrix)

### Hard Mineral System: Coral-Derived Biomineralization

**The mechanism:**
Coral galaxin (Acropora digitifera) nucleates calcium carbonate crystal growth.
Sea urchin sm30/sm50 matrix proteins guide crystal orientation and shape.
Together they can deposit CaCO3 with controlled hardness and geometry.

**What we use this for:**
- Surface hardening of fungal walls over time (the "hardens over 6 months" property)
- Floor surfaces and load-bearing columns
- Water-resistant exterior surfaces

**The challenge:** Coral grows slowly and requires seawater ions. The plan:
Phase 3 — express biomineralization proteins in fungal chassis, provide Ca²⁺ via
substrate supplementation. Decouple the mineral machinery from the coral organism.

### Repair System: Planaria / Axolotl Regeneration Circuits

**The mechanism:**
Planaria piwi pathway maintains a pool of pluripotent stem cells (neoblasts)
throughout the organism. Damage signal → piwi activation → neoblast migration
→ dedifferentiation → regrowth of damaged region.

**What we adapt:**
- Damage-inducible promoter (responds to cellular stress signals)
- Simplified piwi-like circuit for fungal chassis (fungi have no native neoblasts,
  but hyphae already regrow — we amplify and direct this)
- WUS/CLV3 plant meristem circuit for plant-chassis growth renewal

### Directional Growth: Arabidopsis Phototropism / Thigmotropism

**What this provides:**
- Grow toward/away from specific light directions → directional architecture
- Grow along surfaces (thigmotropism) → organic wall formation
- Root-like gravity response → anchor structures into substrate
- WUS/CLV3 feedback → control where new growth initiates

These circuits are in the existing `species_search.py` CAPABILITY_MAP
under "growth". Phase 3 designs the GRNs that deploy them in context.

## The Developmental Program (The Hardest Problem)

Growing a house-shaped structure from a single seeded culture requires solving
morphogenesis: how local rules produce global form.

**The core insight from sea urchin developmental biology (Davidson lab):**
The sea urchin builds a precisely shaped mineral skeleton from a single fertilized egg.
The entire developmental program is encoded as a gene regulatory network (GRN) —
a circuit of transcription factors that activate each other in a spatially specific way.
This is the most completely mapped GRN in biology.

**The plan to adapt this:**

### Stage 1: Scaffold-Guided Growth (Phase 3)
Use a physical scaffold (biodegradable mycelium board) as a substrate that the
living system grows around. The scaffold provides the initial geometric constraints.
The organism fills and reinforces the shape, then the scaffold biodegrades.
This is how Ecovative grows furniture. We use it for the first living wall prototypes.

No morphogenesis required — the shape comes from the scaffold.
The organism's job is structural consolidation and self-repair.

### Stage 2: Gradient-Guided Growth (Phase 4)
Eliminate the physical scaffold. Replace it with:
- Light gradients (designed light environment during growth)
- Chemical gradients in the substrate
- Designed morphogen-secreting seed cells that establish spatial coordinates

The organism uses these gradients as a coordinate system.
This requires the GRN designer (Phase 3) to work at sufficient depth.

### Stage 3: Fully Self-Directing Growth (Phase 5+)
The organism reads its own state and modifies its behavior accordingly.
Internal morphogen gradients, cell-cell signaling, and feedback control
allow complex shapes to emerge from simple seed conditions.

This is the long-term vision and the hardest research problem.
It maps to Phase 4 of the original roadmap (Custom DNA LLM).

## The Custom DNA LLM (The Long-Term Technical Foundation)

All the multi-model complexity of Phases 1-4 generates something invaluable:
**training data**.

Every (English description → biological spec → generated sequence → fitness score) tuple
is a labeled example. Every GRN design, regulatory circuit, and evolutionary refinement run
generates biological signal. The system is training itself as it operates.

**Training corpus for the custom model:**
- JGI MycoCosm: 10,000+ fungal genomes (Ganoderma, Pleurotus, Aspergillus)
- Coral genome databases (Acropora digitifera + zooxanthellae symbiont)
- Planaria/axolotl regeneration transcriptomics
- Arabidopsis developmental atlas (spatial + temporal expression)
- Sea urchin GRN dataset (Davidson lab — the gold standard)
- ENCODE regulatory annotations
- Single-cell RNA-seq atlases from all target organisms
- Every design this system produces, with wet-lab validation feedback

**What the custom model understands that no existing model does:**
- The specific regulatory grammar of structural/regenerative organisms
- The codon dialects of living architecture chassis organisms
- The developmental programs that grow complex multicellular forms
- The connection between sequence, regulation, and 3D spatial structure

**Architecture:** Fine-tune from Nucleotide Transformer or Evo backbone.
The English bridge becomes a lightweight adapter, not a separate system.

This is Phase 4 of the original roadmap. It collapses the multi-model complexity
into a single model that speaks all three biological layers natively.

## The Moral Architecture of the Software

Every phase of this system must pass a moral test.
These are not afterthoughts. They are design constraints.

### 1. Transparency over confidence
Generated designs are always labeled as hypotheses with confidence scores.
The system never presents a candidate as proven. Uncertainty is displayed honestly.
Claude explains what it doesn't know as clearly as what it does.

### 2. Ecological debt awareness
Every design shows its metabolic cost: ATP load, substrate consumption,
ecological impact of the chassis organism. A design that exploits the host
or depletes critical metabolites is flagged, not silenced.

### 3. Voluntarist biology
We design with the organism's logic, not against it. The compatibility engine
exists to catch us when we're imposing rather than collaborating. Designs that
fight the host chassis are restructured, not forced through.

### 4. Equity of access
The GRN database and design engine are open-source.
The platform is tiered for accessibility.
A community without resources should not be locked out of growing their own shelter.
Living architecture that only serves the wealthy repeats the failure of the block-world.

### 5. The human stays in the loop
No sequence is synthesis-ready without human expert review.
The system is a design partner, not an autonomous factory.
Every exported design includes a clear statement of what has and hasn't been validated.

### 6. Ecological restoration as default
Default chassis organism: Ganoderma — ecologically restorative, carbon-sequestering.
Default growth substrate: agricultural waste (mycelium grows on it naturally).
Default end-of-life: biodegradable.
The living house should leave the land better than it found it.

## The Design Philosophy in the Code

The code should reflect the philosophy:
- **Spiral, not block** — modular, composable, emergent from parts rather than
  monolithic. Each module does one thing, invites combination.
- **Stewardship, not control** — the system flags conflicts and explains them,
  rather than silently overriding. The designer always knows what was changed and why.
- **Invite emergence** — design conditions, not outcomes. The morphogenesis engine
  simulates; it does not prescribe the final form.
- **Honest about uncertainty** — confidence scores everywhere. Caveats displayed.
  The system never overclaims.
- **Living documentation** — code comments explain the biology, not just the code.
  A future biologist reading this should understand what it's doing and why.
