# Local Coordinator Onboarding Guide — California

> You weren't recruited. You resonated. That's why you're here.

---

## What Is a Local Coordinator?

You are the root system on your campus. Not a manager. Not a recruiter. A **cultivator.**

Your job is simple: create spaces where people can discover what you discovered — that life organizes itself without permission, and that this insight changes everything about how we think about freedom, community, and the future.

You are part of Students For Liberty, but what you're really part of is a network of people who see the spiral where others see the block.

---

## What You're NOT

- You are not a political operative. You don't need to convert anyone
- You are not a chapter president running meetings with agendas and bylaws
- You are not alone. There's a network of LCs across California, and a State Director (Sage) whose job is to connect you to resources and each other

---

## Your First 30 Days

### Week 1: Orient
- [ ] Join the California LC group chat (link provided by Sage)
- [ ] Read the `tabling_playbook.md` — this is your field manual
- [ ] Identify 2–3 people on your campus who might resonate (roommates, classmates, club members)
- [ ] Walk your campus and find the best tabling spots and event rooms

### Week 2: Connect
- [ ] Have 1-on-1 conversations with those 2–3 people. Use the questions from the tabling playbook — don't pitch, just talk
- [ ] Introduce yourself in the LC group chat. Share what your campus is like
- [ ] Attend the monthly LC call

### Week 3: Plant
- [ ] Set up one small tabling session (even 2 hours). You can do it alone or with one friend
- [ ] Use the spiral questions. See what happens. Note which questions land
- [ ] Share results in the group chat

### Week 4: Reflect
- [ ] What worked? What felt forced? What surprised you?
- [ ] Message Sage with a quick update — honest, not polished
- [ ] Decide: do you want to host a Socratic event next month? (See `socratic_event_template.md`)

## The Spiral Principles (How We Operate)

1. **Grow, don't recruit.** We're not filling quotas. We're finding people who already sense that something about centralized authority doesn't match how life actually works. When they hear the spiral framing, it clicks. That's growth.

2. **Ask, don't tell.** The Socratic method is our primary tool. Questions open minds. Declarations close them. "How does a forest organize itself?" does more work than any pamphlet.

3. **Connect laterally.** You're not reporting up a chain of command. You're sharing across a network. When you learn something, share it with the other LCs. When you need help, ask the network, not just the director.

4. **Model, don't argue.** The Pink Cow principle: be something so real and principled that people follow your direction without you having to convince them. Integrity is the most persuasive force there is.

5. **Water what's alive.** If someone isn't interested, move on gracefully. If someone lights up, invest your time there. Don't chase. Cultivate.

6. **Document what works.** Every conversation, every event, every question that landed — note it. This network learns from itself. Your experience becomes a resource for every LC who comes after you.

## What Success Looks Like

Not metrics. Not sign-up numbers. Not event attendance counts.

Success is:
- **One person** on your campus who sees the world differently because of a conversation you started
- **One event** where someone says "I've never heard it put that way before"
- **One connection** to another LC that creates something neither of you could do alone
- **You** growing as a thinker, speaker, and leader — not for a resume, but because this work matters

Everything else — the metrics, the reports, the institutional stuff — follows naturally from genuine work. Give them the numbers they need. But let the real work drive the numbers, not the other way around.
