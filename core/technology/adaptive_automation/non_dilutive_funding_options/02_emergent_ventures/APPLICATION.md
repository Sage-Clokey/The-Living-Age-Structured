### The Proposal (1500 words max)

**DRAFT:**

**LivingCAD: Open-Source Design Software for the Living Age**

Everything we build is dead. The walls of every house, the frame of every building,
the material of every city — extracted, processed, fixed, degrading. We have accepted
this as a law of nature. It isn't.

Living organisms already produce materials stronger than steel (spider silk), harder
than concrete (coral biomineralization), and more adaptive than any engineered
composite (fungal mycelium that grows around obstacles and repairs damage). The
biology exists. What doesn't exist is the design software.

I am building LivingCAD — the first organism-scale biodesign platform that translates
plain English descriptions of desired biological function into synthesizable DNA
sequences, codon-optimized for structural organisms. You type: "design a protein that
cross-links fungal cell walls under mechanical stress." The system interprets the
biological intent (via Claude, Anthropic's LLM), generates candidate protein sequences
(via ESM-3, EvolutionaryScale's protein language model), optimizes the DNA for the
target organism's codon dialect, validates against a compatibility engine checking
regulatory conflicts and pathway bottlenecks, and explains the result in plain English.

**What already exists:**

This is not a pitch deck. The foundation is working code:
- Genome retrieval system querying 220+ genomes from UCSC and NCBI
- Codon optimization engine with validated RSCU tables for 5 chassis organisms
  (Ganoderma lucidum, yeast, Arabidopsis, human, Komagataeibacter)
- Regulatory element compatibility analysis (24 characterized parts, 5 organism classes)
- Pathway conflict detection (12 metabolic/signaling profiles)
- Adaptive assembly engine
- Claude API integration translating English → structured biological specifications

The ESM-3 bridge — connecting English to protein generation — is the next piece.

**Why this advances liberty:**

The design tools for biological systems are being built by a small number of
well-funded companies — Ginkgo Bioworks, defense-adjacent synthetic biology firms —
as proprietary platforms. If these tools remain closed, the ability to design living
materials will be concentrated in the hands of whoever can afford platform access.

LivingCAD's core — the compatibility engine, the GRN database, the bridge layer — will
be open source. The Red Hat model: open infrastructure, commercial platform on top.
Any iGEM team, any small lab, any architect experimenting with mycelium, any researcher
in any country gets access to the same design tools as a team at MIT.

This matters because the most important designs won't come from corporate labs. They'll
come from independent researchers, small teams, and builders in places where shipping
concrete is impossible but growing mycelium is free.

**The path:**

Phase 1 (now): English → protein → DNA pipeline. Prove the bridge works.
Phase 2: Regulatory layer (when/where genes activate) using Enformer.
Phase 3: Developmental programs (how form emerges) from curated GRN databases.
Phase 4: Custom DNA LLM trained on living architecture data.
Phase 5: Full living architecture system — speak a structure into existence.

Each phase produces usable software sold to synthetic biology researchers
($50-200/month academic, $500-2,000/month biotech). Revenue funds the moonshot.
The Falcon 9 funding Starship.
