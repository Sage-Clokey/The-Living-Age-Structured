Jay Keasling's artemisinin work at Berkeley → Amyris is the template. George Church's lab has spun out 20+ companies. This is a well-worn path in synbio.

## Path 3: Skip the PhD, Build the Company

**What you already have:**
- The Adaptive Genome Design System (compatibility engine, pathway profiles, codon harmonization)
- The theoretical framework (capstone paper, GLS concept paper)
- A GitHub presence and portfolio

**What you'd need:**
- Wet lab validation (the 10-block proof of concept)
- Funding for lab access

**Funding options:**
- **SBIR/STTR Phase I** — $275K, 6-12 months, no PhD required. NSF and DOE both fund synthetic biology. The GLS concept is novel enough for an SBIR.
- **IndieBio** — SF-based biotech accelerator, $250K for 15% equity, gives you lab space and mentorship. They've funded synbio startups at this stage.
- **Y Combinator** — has funded bio companies (Ginkgo started here). You'd need a co-founder with wet lab skills.
- **Nucleate** — student-run biotech venture program, helps you go from idea to fundable company. Chapters at most research universities.

**Pros:** Speed, you own everything, no advisor gatekeeping
**Cons:** No lab access without funding, harder to publish, credibility gap without PhD in this field

## Path 4: The Recommended Hybrid

### Year 1 (now → Summer 2027)

1. Apply to PhD programs this fall (Boeke at NYU, Church at Harvard, Keasling at Berkeley, Ellis at Imperial). Use the capstone + GLS paper as writing samples.
2. Simultaneously apply for an NSF GRFP (Graduate Research Fellowship) — $37K/year for 3 years, portable to any lab. The GLS framework is a strong application because it's interdisciplinary (economics + synthetic biology + bioinformatics).
3. File a provisional patent on the GLS compatibility engine before starting — costs ~$200, gives 12 months of IP protection. The code is already written and owned.

### Years 2-4 (in PhD program)

1. Build the 10-block prokaryotic proof of concept in the advisor's lab
2. Publish the experimental validation
3. Convert provisional patent to full patent (the university's tech transfer office helps with this)
4. Present at SynBioBeta, GP-write meetings, ASM — build the network

### Year 4-5

1. Apply for SBIR Phase I through a university spin-out entity
2. Use PhD results as the preliminary data
3. Defend dissertation
4. Spin out the company with license to the patent from the university

### Why this path:
- The PhD gives you the lab, the credibility, and the publications
- The provisional patent protects the idea before sharing it with an advisor
- The SBIR comes after experimental data exists, making it much more competitive
- You graduate with a PhD, publications, a patent, and a funded company
