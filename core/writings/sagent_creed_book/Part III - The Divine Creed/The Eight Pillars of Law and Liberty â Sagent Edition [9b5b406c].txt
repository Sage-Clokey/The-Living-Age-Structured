Overview

Islamic law views wealth not as domination, but as stewardship under God. The Qur’an forbids riba (usury) — profit without shared risk — and calls for trade founded on fairness, consent, and trust. Islamic banking replaces debt with partnership, ensuring that gains and losses are shared justly. Through zakat (charity) and ethical commerce, wealth circulates in righteousness, purifying both giver and receiver. True justice is achieved when prosperity flows through honesty and balance, guided by divine law.

“Force is moral only when used to defend life, liberty, and property.” — The Defender’s Creed
