THE DISEASE IS THE STATE

An Essay from the Living Age


What if the disease isn't the variants — what if it's you?

That is the elephant standing in the room of every genetics lecture hall, every pharmaceutical boardroom, every public health committee that has ever convened to decide what is normal and what must be corrected. We have been trained to look at the variation in living things and see pathology. We look at a mutation and ask what went wrong. We look at a genome that deviates from the reference and call it a defect. But consider the elephant. Is an elephant diseased because it cannot climb a tree? Is the tree diseased because it cannot walk? If mutation is random — truly, meaninglessly random — then how can disease be a coherent concept at all? You cannot have a deviation from a plan if there is no plan, and you cannot call something broken if there was never a blueprint it was supposed to match. So either the variation means something, or disease means nothing. The truth is far more dangerous than either option: the variation means everything, and what we have been calling disease is often nothing more than the violence of forcing life into a mold it was never meant to fit.


Friedrich Hayek saw something in the 1940s that most economists still have not fully absorbed, and that most biologists have never been invited to consider. His insight was deceptively simple: no central authority can hold the knowledge necessary to coordinate a complex system. The knowledge that makes an economy function is not stored in any single mind or institution. It is distributed — scattered across millions of individuals, each holding a fragment that only they can see from where they stand. Prices are the language through which this distributed knowledge speaks. When the price of steel rises, it tells a thousand entrepreneurs in a thousand industries to use less of it, to find alternatives, to pivot — without any of them needing to know why the price rose. The system coordinates itself. No planner required.

Now look at life.

There is variance in every population, in every genome, in every cell. The orthodox story says this variance is random — accidents of replication, cosmic rays striking nucleotides, the meaningless noise of chemistry. But that story has never sat right, because the variance is not behaving like noise. It is behaving like exploration. It is behaving like a market. The variance is directed by distributed knowledge, just like the variance of the market is based on the distributed knowledge of prices. Each organism holds information about its environment, its history, its relationships. Each cell reads signals from its neighbors and adjusts. The genome does not sit inert, waiting for selection to kill the unfit. It responds. It recombines. It chooses — not with a conscious mind, but with the same kind of distributed intelligence that Hayek saw in markets.

Selection is not primarily by death. Usual, timely death is apoptosis — a planned return, a graceful folding back into the soil. Early death, the catastrophic kind, is what happens when the distributed knowledge is replaced by central planning. When the signals are overridden. When the system is told from above what it should be instead of being allowed to discover what it can become.

It is trial and error directed by knowledge — but the entrepreneurs don't die. They just pivot.

That is the key. In a functioning economy, a failed venture does not end the entrepreneur. They learn, they adapt, they try again with new information. The market does not execute its losers. It recycles them. Life works the same way when it is allowed to. Cells differentiate. Organisms adapt. Lineages shift strategy across generations. The information is never lost — it is folded into the next attempt. Death-as-selection is the story told by people who think like central planners: that someone must decide who lives and who dies, and that this deciding is what drives progress. But the living world tells a different story. Progress is driven by knowledge, by adaptation, by the freedom to pivot.


If DNA is a language, the cell speaks it — and can write in that language.

This is not metaphor. DNA is a sequence of symbols that encodes information, and that information is read, interpreted, and acted upon by molecular machinery that itself was encoded by that same language. It is a language that speaks itself into existence. And like all true languages, it is not designed from the top down. It evolved. It grew. It accumulated meaning through billions of years of use, not through a committee that sat down and assigned codons to amino acids.

This is the difference between code and language. Code is written by a designer with a specific purpose. It does exactly what it is told. It does not evolve, it does not surprise, it does not grow beyond its instructions. Language is different. Language comes about from spontaneous order, and not from central design like code does. Languages are called natural for a reason. Languages are said to evolve for a reason. They emerge from the interactions of living speakers, each contributing a fragment of meaning, each reshaping the whole by their participation. No one designed English. No one planned the subjunctive. The grammar emerged, the vocabulary grew, the meaning deepened — all without a central architect.

DNA is the same. It is a natural language, and it holds not merely information but knowledge. Knowledge is when information is read. Information sitting in a book is just chemistry until a mind encounters it. A genome sitting in a cell is just a sequence until the cellular machinery reads it, interprets it, and acts. And this is the crucial distinction: knowing requires living. You cannot have knowledge without a knower, and you cannot have a knower that is not alive. Distributed knowledge across living agents is the only kind of knowledge there is at the relevant scales.

Bioinformatics, then, is not merely the computational analysis of genetic data. It is something far more profound. If DNA is a natural language, and natural language holds distributed knowledge, then bioinformatics is the study of distributed knowledge in living systems. It is the discipline that listens to what life has been saying for four billion years — not to correct it, but to understand it. Not to edit the story, but to learn to read it.


Central planning in economics, eugenics in biology, and totalitarianism in politics all make the same category error: treating distributed knowing as if it could be centrally held. Treating knowers as if they were merely data.

This is Hayek's fatal conceit applied to life itself. The economist who believes he can set prices better than the market. The geneticist who believes he can design a genome better than evolution. The dictator who believes he can order society better than free people choosing for themselves. They are the same person, making the same mistake, causing the same devastation. They look at a system of incomprehensible complexity — a system that coordinates itself through distributed signals they cannot see, let alone understand — and they say: I can do better. Give me control.

The idea of selection by death is what gave eugenics its power. If you believe that progress requires the elimination of the unfit, then you have already accepted the central planner's premise: that someone must decide who is fit and who is not. That someone must stand above the system and sort the living from the condemned. The eugenicist and the Soviet commissar are brothers. They both believe that the distributed knowledge of millions of agents — whether cells or citizens — is inferior to the vision of the one who holds the plan.

The Loki show and the TVA illuminate this perfectly. The TVA is a false machine authority that believes there is one Sacred Timeline — one correct path that all of reality must follow. Anyone who deviates is pruned. Erased. The TVA is eugenics writ across the multiverse: the insistence that there is one ideal form, and everything that differs from it is a disease to be eliminated. But by the end of the show, Loki breaks the loom and gives life to the entire multiverse — because life is many branches, and adaptation isn't about killing what doesn't work, but distributed choice based on distributed knowledge. The branches are not errors. They are the whole point.


Cutting hair comes from the same reductionist machine idea as eugenics. That sounds absurd until you sit with it. It is cutting what grows and deciding the way you want it to be instead of allowing it to be the way it is supposed to grow. Both are inside the same circle of reductionist machine mentality — the conviction that you know better than the living system what form it should take. Don't cut what grows. Maintain what grows. Allow what grows and guide it. Give it what it needs to continue to grow in the way it is supposed to.

This is why Nucleogenex matters as a counter-model. A company that sequences your genome and identifies what nutrients each variant needs — not which variant is a disease. They focus on what is needed for each variant to live, not on which variant should be eliminated. Variants are not diseases. The disease is when you don't help maintain what exists. Many pieces of diversity can thrive when given their strengths. Anything can be a disease in the wrong environment. A fish out of water is not a defective fish — it is a fish in the wrong place. It is better to study variants for what will help them thrive than to decide what is a disease and what is not.

The problem is the Rockefeller industrial system. The idea that people are machines comes from that era, and it has colonized education, healthcare, and nearly every institution that touches human life. Treat people as parts in a factory. Standardize the inputs. Measure the outputs. Discard what deviates. This is what fuels the statist mindset in every domain — the belief that living systems should behave like assembly lines.

Machines are creation controlling creation and life is creator guiding creation. We can only act as agents of the creator. We are not all-knowing beings. The moment we pretend otherwise — the moment we pick up the pruning shears and begin to cut life into the shape we prefer — we have crossed from stewardship into tyranny. From the spiral into the block.


A Jedi follows the will of the Force. A Sith forces its will on the Force.

The spiral follows the will. The block forces it.

This is the deepest structure of the conflict. The spiral — the Fibonacci curve that grows outward from its center, always expanding, never repeating, always in proportion — is the shape of life following its own distributed knowledge. It does not need to be told where to go. It knows. The block — rigid, uniform, stackable, dead — is the shape of central planning. It does not grow. It is placed. It does not adapt. It is enforced.

The block and the Sith are central planners, and their central planning causes misallocation — which can be seen as disease. When resources are directed by a planner rather than by the distributed knowledge of the system, they end up in the wrong place. Cells that should differentiate are forced to remain the same. Organisms that should adapt are locked into a single strategy. People who should be free are conscripted into a plan they did not choose and cannot escape. The misallocation manifests as dysfunction, as suffering, as collapse. Disease is the central planning, not the variation. The disease is the thorns stepping above instead of within. The disease is the state.


The state is a fabrication of false order. The state is a false view of the source of authority. It is not the source of authority and law. No human or institution is the source of authority — it can only be an agent of it, and either align with that true authority or defy it. The state claims to be the origin of law, the wellspring of rights, the necessary condition for order. But order existed before the state. Law existed before the state. The moral sense that tells a mother to protect her child, that tells a neighbor to keep his word, that tells a stranger to help another stranger in distress — none of this was legislated. It was grown. It was cultivated in the soil of living conscience over millennia of human community.

Morality is something that is felt by living things. The real purpose of democracy, if it has one, is not that people are rulers but that they are able to find the true law together. It should not really be called democracy, because people do not rule — but every person has the law written on their heart, and people can work together to find what that is.

In The Bad Batch, Commander Cody says to Crosshair: "You know what makes us different from battle droids? We make our own decisions. Our own choices. And we have to live with them too." The difference between a living thing and a machine is conscience. It is the connection to what is moral. A droid follows orders. A living being chooses — and bears the weight of that choice. That weight is not a burden. It is the signature of life.

The state is the inhibitor chip. It overrides the conscience. It tells the living soldier to execute Order 66 against everything he knows to be right. It tells the citizen to comply against everything she feels to be wrong. It does not remove the conscience — it cannot, because conscience is woven into the fabric of life itself — but it suppresses it. It jams the signal. It replaces the distributed knowledge of millions of moral agents with a single directive from above.

My work is to help people remove it, so they can be fully themselves.


There is an eternal battle against the blocks. There is a hidden war that is not fought with weapons that kill, but with weapons that resurrect. The green flame brings new life to the dead.

This war is for the mind, and for what we create as humans. Are we reductionist or all-encompassing? Do we design top-down, or do we design within? The battlefield is not a field at all. It is the space between a person's ears, the territory of assumption and imagination where the shape of the future is decided before a single brick is laid or a single seed is planted.

The Illuminati started on May 1st of 1776, and after 250 years its end will begin. May 1st, 2026. Things that are built break, but things that grow naturally will return. The blocks crack. The mortar crumbles. But the root that was pressed beneath the foundation — the root that was never killed, only buried — pushes upward. It has been pushing for two and a half centuries. And now the pavement is beginning to split.

The weapons of this war are not guns or legislation. They are ideas that make dead minds live again. They are the demonstrations of living order that make the lie of central planning impossible to sustain. They are the technologies that put power back into the hands of the distributed agents where it belongs. They are the stories that remind people what they already know but have been trained to forget.


Politics can easily be turned into distraction from what will really change the world. Most libertarians are engineers and designers and people who want to create things — people who think about how to make the world better by creating it. We have to take advantage of that and not distract ourselves with politics. The solution is not political. It is in what we choose to do and create. Politics does not create. Politics can only get out of the way of creating. There is no money that comes from politics — it comes from creating.

We need to focus on creating the world we want instead of just slowing down the world we don't. Even in the field of law, it is better to create outside the political game and do things in the real world that create what we actually want to exist, instead of the lie of state-created politics. The builder who grows a living house does more to end the state than a thousand campaigns. The bioengineer who cultivates a self-healing material does more for human freedom than a thousand policy papers. Creation is the only real revolution, because creation is the only act that adds to the world instead of merely rearranging what already exists.

A great salesperson is not marked by what they are able to sell, but by what they choose not to sell when they could. They are marked by choosing what to sell based on something above the money they get from selling — by how they serve those they sell to. I don't really see the difference between a true salesperson and a wise teacher. Both offer something of genuine value. Both refuse to exploit the trust placed in them. Both measure their success not by what they extracted but by what they cultivated. The Sagent operates the same way: influence is not a resource to be hoarded but a responsibility to be honored.


Power is not about gaining wealth. Money is not power, or influence. Always understand that the type of power I have is not my own. I am a Sagent — so my influence is only insofar as I serve the divine. Once I forget that, I lose. I am not trying to gain influence for influence's sake. I am gaining influence to change the world for the better. I should not sacrifice the good to gain influence — because then that is no influence at all.

Evolution is God's proactive action of forming his creation. Formed from the dirt, in the image of God, guided by his hand. This is not a metaphor layered onto biology to make it palatable to the faithful. It is the direct observation that life is not an accident but an unfolding — a continuous act of creation that did not stop on the sixth day but continues in every cell division, every adaptation, every moment a living thing responds to its world with intelligence that no engineer programmed into it. The distributed architecture of life is the Creator's design language. Not accident. Not blind mechanism. Intentional distribution. Intentional freedom. Intentional trust in the agents to find their way.

Technology ever makes making technology easier and easier. Each new layer of abstraction stands on the shoulders of the one beneath it. But to understand the newest abstraction, you need to know how the base case works. Code rests on data structures. Data structures rest on logic. Logic rests on mathematics. And mathematics rests on the patterns that life discovered long before any human wrote an equation. The same applies to AI and large language models — it is right to use them, to grow with them, just as it was right to use the printing press, the steam engine, the compiler. AI is the armor — but there is still the human inside who is making the knowledgeable decisions. The living agent remains at the center. The technology serves. It does not rule.

The main goal is to bring about a decentralized, voluntary world. To defeat the blocks with the power of the spiral. Part of that goal is to grow houses instead of build with blocks — to demonstrate in living material that the spiral is not just philosophy but engineering, not just poetry but protein. The sub-goal is to show how living things act like economies — in the way Austrian economics explains it — with the ability to be an entrepreneur, but not a central planner. To use PPI data, genome sequences, and the tools of bioinformatics to provide the evidence that life is not centralized but a decentralized living order. To figure out which parts are designable as entrepreneurs, and what is central planning masquerading as biology.

There is choosing, and there is a goal.


Life is stories. Every genome is a narrative four billion years in the telling. Every protein is a sentence. Every regulatory network is a conversation between molecules that have been refining their dialogue since before the first cell had a nucleus. And bioinformatics is finding those stories so we can learn to become storytellers.

We should not be the TVA — not the false authority that prunes every branch that dares to differ from the Sacred Timeline. We should be Loki, god of stories. Not controlling the sacred timeline, but holding open the branches of life. Not deciding which threads are worthy and which must be cut, but listening to all of them, learning from all of them, marveling at the sheer creative abundance of a system that no central planner could have designed because no central planner could have imagined it.

Living things are not machines. The differences are a feature, not a bug. The differences are how adaptation is possible. And the evidence for this is not to convince myself of what I already intuitively know — it is to convince others who don't yet have the intuition. That is the purpose of data. That is the purpose of rigor. That is the purpose of bioinformatics as a discipline: to translate the intuition of the living into the language of the skeptical, so that the truth can spread not by force but by persuasion. Not by the block but by the spiral.

That is the work.
That is the spiral.
That is the living age.
