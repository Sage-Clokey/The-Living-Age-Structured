"I Now Take a Longer-Term View on People." — Steve Jobs

This statement reflects a shift in perspective, a deeper understanding of human nature, growth, and the long game of influence.

In a world obsessed with instant results, quick judgments, and surface-level interactions, Steve Jobs' realization points to a more profound truth: People are not defined by a single moment, a single action, or even a single failure.

To truly understand someone, you have to zoom out—to see them not as they are now, but as they evolve over time.


---

1. The Short-Term Illusion: Judging Too Quickly

Most people judge others based on their current status, immediate impact, or momentary performance.

They see failure as permanent, mistakes as defining, and potential as static.

This is short-term thinking—it assumes that people don’t change, don’t grow, and don’t surprise us.


But in reality:

The people who fail today may lead tomorrow.

The ones who seem lost now may be the visionaries of the future.

The person who isn’t valuable to you yet may become your greatest ally later.


Steve Jobs himself was fired from Apple. If people had judged him in that moment, they would have called him a failure, a lost cause, an irrelevant genius.
But with a longer-term view, that exile became the exact experience he needed to return stronger than ever and revolutionize technology.

Short-term thinking would have dismissed him.
Long-term thinking saw what he could become.


---

2. The Power of Patience: People Grow, Evolve, and Prove Themselves Over Time

A longer-term view on people means:

Recognizing that someone’s current state does not define their future.

Understanding that the seeds of greatness take time to bloom.

Trusting that if someone is on the right path, even if they stumble, they may still change the world.


Examples of Long-Term Thinking on People:

Elon Musk was laughed at for Tesla and SpaceX. Now, he leads both industries.

J.K. Rowling was rejected by multiple publishers. Now, she’s one of the most successful authors ever.

Michael Jordan was cut from his high school basketball team. Now, he’s the greatest of all time.


If you judge people in the moment, you miss their potential.
If you judge people over time, you see their evolution.


---

3. The Key to Leadership: Investing in People Before They "Deserve" It

One of the most powerful aspects of leadership is believing in people before they believe in themselves.

Great leaders do not just find talent—they nurture it, refine it, and give it space to develop.

Instead of asking, “What can this person do for me today?”, they ask, “Who can this person become in 5 years?”


Steve Jobs understood that talent is not always obvious at first.

Some people need time to prove their value.

Some need failures to sharpen their skills.

Some need opportunities they don’t yet realize they are ready for.


Taking a longer-term view on people means investing in the unseen, the unrealized, and the underestimated.


---

4. The Ultimate Truth: Long-Term Thinking Wins Every Time

Most people are playing the short game—chasing quick rewards, making snap judgments, and focusing only on immediate results.
The ones who play the long game—whether in business, relationships, or personal growth—are the ones who truly change the world.

If you take a longer-term view on people:

You won’t dismiss someone because of temporary failures.

You’ll recognize potential where others only see flaws.

You’ll build relationships that last, rather than ones that serve a momentary purpose.


Final Thought:

The world underestimates people in the short term and overestimates them in the long term.
The key is to see beyond the moment—to recognize not just who someone is, but who they are becoming.
