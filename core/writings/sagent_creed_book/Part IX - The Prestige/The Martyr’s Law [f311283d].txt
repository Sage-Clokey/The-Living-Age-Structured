The Martyr’s Law

Martyrs are not remembered for how they died —
but for what refused to die with them.

Their bodies fall,
but their truth does not collapse.
Their names fade from headlines,
but their message echoes louder in silence.
The empire strikes them down,
only to discover resistance has grown tenfold.

A martyr is not the end of a movement —
but the moment it begins to remember itself.

For tyrants can kill a man,
but they cannot kill the hunger for freedom
that he awakened.

When a storyteller is silenced,
stories spread like wildfire.
When a truth-speaker is buried,
seeds of rebellion crack open underground.
When a shepherd falls,
the flock becomes an army.

Death is their amplifier.
Legacy is their victory.
Truth is their resurrection.

This is why the empire fears the ones who cannot be bought:
If they strike them down,
they shall become more powerful
than tyranny could possibly imagine.
