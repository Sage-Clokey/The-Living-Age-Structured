# Stossel Fellowship — 1 Minute Video Script

**Speaker:** Sage Clokey
**Format:** Direct to camera, no cuts, honest delivery
**Target length:** 55–60 seconds

---

## SCRIPT

John, you've spent decades making the invisible hand visible. You took economics — something most people think is boring — and turned it into stories people could see. That's exactly what I want to do with biology.

I just graduated from UC Santa Cruz with a degree in bioinformatics. I study how living systems organize themselves. And here's what nobody in the liberty movement is talking about:

Your body is 37 trillion cells. No president. No congress. No central plan. They coordinate through local signals, voluntary exchange, and distributed knowledge — exactly the way Hayek described a free market. And when one cell overrides that system — when it centralizes control and demands all resources flow to itself — we have a word for that. Cancer.

This isn't a metaphor. This is peer-reviewed molecular biology. Genomes don't even evolve by random accident — they restructure themselves from the bottom up, rearranging existing parts in new combinations. Life is an engineer, not a lottery.

The strongest case for liberty isn't in an economics textbook. It's in every living thing on earth. And no one is putting it on camera.

I'm also the grandson of Art Clokey — the man who created Gumby. He showed the world that clay could come alive. I want to show the world that the science of life proves what the liberty movement has been arguing in the wrong language for decades.

You make economics visual. I want to help you make biology visual. Because life itself is the proof that voluntary order works.

---

## DELIVERY NOTES

- Speak like you're talking to someone you respect, not performing
- Hold the camera like a conversation, not a pitch
- The cancer line is the hook — let it land, don't rush past it
- End with conviction, not pleading — you're offering something, not asking for a favor
- Film in natural light if possible — no ring light, no studio look — you're from the Shire, not the tower
