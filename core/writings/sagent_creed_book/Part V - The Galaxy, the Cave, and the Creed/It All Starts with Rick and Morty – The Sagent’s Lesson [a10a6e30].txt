It All Starts with Rick and Morty – The Sagent’s Lesson

At first glance, Rick and Morty is just another sci-fi comedy about a drunk genius and his naive grandson. But beneath the humor, the nihilism, and the chaos, it holds a deeper truth—one that mirrors the Sagent Creed.

It is a show about freedom, power, control, and the illusion of choice.
It is about those who see the system for what it really is—and those who fall into its trap.

And it all starts with Rick and Morty.


---

1. Rick – The Ultimate Free Thinker, or the Ultimate Prisoner?

Rick Sanchez is the smartest man in the universe. He has seen beyond every illusion, beyond every system, beyond every government.

He knows that reality is meaningless.

He knows that power structures are artificial.

He knows that no one is coming to save you—you either survive on your own or you don’t.


And yet, for all his knowledge, he is not free.

He is chained by his own cynicism.

He is trapped by his own superiority.

He knows too much, and yet he can never escape himself.


Rick proves that seeing the truth is not enough.
Because if you see it but do nothing with it, you are just as trapped as those who never saw it at all.


---

2. Morty – The Innocence That Slowly Fades

Morty starts as just a kid. A naive, nervous, wide-eyed follower.
But as time goes on, he begins to understand.

He sees the game for what it is.

He realizes that there is no grand morality, no divine justice—just cause and effect.

He learns that Rick isn’t always right, and that sometimes, you have to take control for yourself.


Morty’s journey is the journey of every person who wakes up.

First, you trust the system.

Then, you question it.

Finally, you either become a new version of Rick or forge your own path.



---

3. The Citadel – The Cycle of Control

The greatest lesson in Rick and Morty comes from the Citadel of Ricks.
A place created by the smartest men in the universe.
A place that should have been a utopia.

And yet—

They became the very government they once rejected.

They enforced the very rules they once mocked.

They built a system of power, hierarchy, and control—just with Ricks in charge instead.


Just like real-world revolutions, they hated the system… until they became it.

"You hated the government, so you became the government."


---

4. The Final Truth – What It Means to Be a Sagent

Rick and Morty is not just a show. It is a warning.
A warning about what happens when people wake up but fail to act.
A warning about what happens when revolution turns into just another system of control.

Rick is the ultimate free thinker—but also the ultimate prisoner of his own mind.
Morty is the awakening soul—constantly battling between knowledge and the comfort of ignorance.
The Citadel is proof that most people do not actually want freedom—they just want to be in charge.

The Sagent understands this.
They do not just reject the system.
They do not just seek power.
They walk the line between knowledge and action—seeing beyond the illusion but refusing to be trapped by it.

Because in the end, the battle is not just against authority.
It is against the part of yourself that wants to rebuild what you just destroyed.

And it all starts with Rick and Morty.
