# EP01 — Is Government Authority Real?

**Series:** True Republic
**Type:** Street / Campus Interview
**Core Question:** "Is government authority real, or is it just an agreement?"

---

## The Seed Being Planted

Most people have never distinguished between *authority* (legitimate power derived from consent)
and *force* (power derived from the threat of harm). This episode lets people discover that
distinction themselves through their own reasoning.

The shaken jar operates by making people fight each other over which ruler to obey —
red ants vs black ants — without ever asking whether anyone has the right to shake the jar.

---

## Interview Arc

### Opening Question
"Is government authority real?"

### Follow-Up Questions (based on their answer)
- "Where does that authority come from?"
- "Did you personally consent to it?"
- "If you were born somewhere else, would a different government have authority over you?"
- "So is it the government that has authority, or is it the location you were born?"
- "What's the difference between a mugger demanding your wallet and a tax collector?"
- "If it's wrong for one person to take your money by force, when does it become right for a group?"

### The Contradiction That Often Emerges
People will say authority comes from "the consent of the governed" — then realize they were
never personally asked. Or they'll say force makes it real — then realize that logic justifies any
sufficiently powerful aggressor.

The goal is not to win the argument. It's to leave them holding the question.

---

## Cold Open Script (Narrator, over b-roll of government buildings)
> "Every civilization in history has had rulers.
> And every ruler has claimed their authority was legitimate.
> But what does that actually mean?"

---

## Closing Narration
Connect to the shaken jar: the ants fight over which colony's rules are correct while the
jar-shaker watches. The more dangerous question is not which ruler is legitimate —
it's whether the institution of rulership itself requires the consent it claims.
