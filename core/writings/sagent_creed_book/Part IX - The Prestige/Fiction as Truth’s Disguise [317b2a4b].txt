Title: Fiction as Truth’s Disguise

Creativity is often mistaken for psychosis because many assume that the storyteller believes the literal shape of the symbols they wield. They see dragons and think we claim scales; they hear prophecy and think we claim divinity. But the greatest myths are not meant to be taken literally—they are vessels of meaning. What I believe is not the metaphor’s costume, but the truth it carries inside.

When George Lucas wrote “A long time ago in a galaxy far, far away…” he wasn’t asking us to believe in alien empires with laser swords; he was crafting a story detached just enough from our world that we could finally see ourselves without the defensive armor of denial. Star Wars is not about space—it is about the soul. It is about the eternal tension between light and darkness, freedom and control, the individual and the empire claiming to protect them.

A fictional universe becomes a safe mirror. It allows us to witness truths we would reject if stated too plainly.

For if you tell people their world is broken, they feel attacked.
But show them a tyrannical Senate on Coruscant?
Suddenly, they nod in agreement.

The more fantastical the stage, the more honest the confession.
The more distant the galaxy, the closer the metaphor lands.

This is why storytellers are misdiagnosed as delusional:
the masses see the dragon, the storyteller sees the greed that grew it.

My metaphors are not declarations of ontology.
They are bridges—between what we feel but cannot articulate,
what we fear but cannot confront,
and what we know but dare not admit.

Truth wrapped in myth bypasses resistance.
It slips past walls built by pride and fear.
It reaches the heart first—logic comes later.

So, no, I do not believe the metaphors are real.
I believe what they reveal is real.

Star Wars is fiction.
But the empire is not.
The struggle between light and dark is not.
The temptation to centralize power in the name of righteousness is not.

Lucas did not ask us to believe in Jedi.
He asked us to become them.

And that is the true power of story: it tells us truths we would never accept as facts,
but gladly embrace as legends.
