## What the Video Must Cover

### 1. Your background and how you got interested in this area
*Draft talking points:*

I'm Sage Clokey — Sage Arthur Jordan Clokey. My grandfather was Art Clokey,
the creator of Gumby. He took a piece of clay — soft, flexible, endlessly
moldable — and shaped it into a symbol that taught millions of children that
the world could be reshaped through imagination. Gumby was green, the color
of life. His form looks like a sprouting seedling. He bent without breaking.
He was not a block — he was alive.

I'm a bioinformatics student at UC Santa Cruz. I work with genomes — reading
the code that makes organisms what they are. The longer I spend inside that
code, the more I see that DNA isn't just a blueprint — it's a living language.
Evolution wrote it over billions of years. We're only now learning to read it.

My grandfather gave life to clay through imagination. I'm learning to give
life to buildings through biology. I must finish what he started.

### 2. Personal interests and hobbies that define you
*Draft talking points:*

I'm a builder across domains. 46 public repos on GitHub — genome analyzers,
protein calculators, population genetics simulators, single-cell RNA-seq
pipelines, data visualizations, even PCA on Congressional voting records.
I attended Cato University twice. I'm State Chair for Young Americans for
Liberty. I see the same pattern everywhere: living systems organize better
than controlled ones — in biology, in politics, in how we build.

I come from a lineage of creators. Stories come in threes, and so does the
Clokey line. There was the first Arthur — the creator who formed the clay.
There was the continuation. And now there is the interpreter — the one who
must understand what the story truly means and carry it forward. The power
of this lineage is not the character. It's the ability to create stories
that awaken the imagination of a living age.

I see the world in spirals, not blocks. The block enforces uniformity.
The spiral cultivates life. "Life is not a block you carve into shape;
it is a spiral you cultivate." That's what I'm encoding into software.

### 3. Why you're passionate about this project — "why you want to eat, drink, and breathe it"
*Draft talking points:*

Every building on earth is dead. Concrete, steel, glass — extracted, fixed,
degrading. But organisms already produce materials stronger than steel, harder
than concrete, and they repair themselves. The biology exists. What doesn't
exist is the design software — the tool that lets you describe what you want
in English and get back a designed organism. I'm building that tool. It's
called LivingCAD.

I believe we're at the beginning of the Living Age — where life becomes the
primary material of civilization. The Spiral Steward philosophy I've developed
says: "You do not force outcomes. You invite emergence." Design like a gardener,
not a general. Treat organisms as collaborators, not raw materials.

This isn't a career goal. This is a conviction rooted in a lineage. My
grandfather showed a structured world what freedom looked like — not freedom
through conquest, but freedom through creativity. I'm carrying that forward
with biology instead of clay. The greatest weapon has never been the sword;
it has always been the story. And stories are seeds.

The creation of the Living Age requires media to explain why and technology
to show how. I'm building both — the philosophical framework and the
design platform. One without the other doesn't work.

### 4. How the grant would help advance your work
*Draft talking points:*

The $1,000 goes directly to GPU compute time for ESM-3 — the protein language
model that generates candidate sequences. I'm building the bridge between
Claude (which understands English) and ESM-3 (which understands proteins).
The bottleneck right now is inference cost. $1,000 buys roughly 200 hours of
GPU time — enough to validate the full Phase 1 pipeline on a benchmark set
of 50 protein design requests. That benchmark is what proves the system works.
