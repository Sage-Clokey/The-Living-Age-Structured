The Exodus of Gumby

An Allegory of Creation, Captivity, and Return
The pages that follow are an allegory. They are not a claim that the biblical story is literally about Gumby, nor that the characters described here are historical equivalents. Rather, they explore recurring patterns that appear across history, art, and human experience. History asks, What happened? Allegory asks, What pattern does this reveal?
Clay

Gumby is a figure of clay.

In Scripture, humanity begins with clay. Adam is formed from the dust of the earth by God. From Adam comes Abraham, who is called to leave his homeland and journey toward the promised land. Through Isaac, Jacob, and the twelve tribes, a people are formed—not only by ancestry, but by covenant with their Creator.

Art Clokey shaped Gumby from clay. He gave him character, imagination, kindness, and wonder. Gumby was never merely a product. He was an expression of a creative vision.

The Recurring Pattern

When famine comes, Israel journeys to Egypt. At first Egypt is a refuge. Through Joseph it is a place of provision. But after Joseph dies, a generation arises that no longer remembers him. What was once a sanctuary becomes a place of slavery.

This pattern repeats throughout history:

A living creation is born.
It grows.
It enters systems that preserve it.
Then those systems forget why it exists.
The form survives. The spirit becomes hidden.
Then comes the call to restoration.

Gumby follows this pattern. Over time, the vision passed through many hands. The character endured, but stewardship became increasingly separated from the original creative purpose.

In this allegory, Egypt is not a nation. Egypt is every place where a living creation becomes disconnected from the purpose for which it was made.

The Unified Vision

It began with those who came before me. My grandfather, Art Clokey, created Gumby from clay. My father, Joe Clokey, devoted his life to carrying that creative vision forward. Around them grew a collection of seemingly different works, yet I see them as expressions of a common purpose:

Gumby explored the relationship between life and the machine.

Davey and Goliath explored many religions and one faith.

San Luis Video Publishing sought to educate people for a future built upon living systems.

The Live Oak Music Festival celebrated authentic human creativity and community.

To me, these were not isolated projects. They were different expressions of a single vision that I call the Living Age.

The Fall

There was a time when these ideas still existed together. Then they became scattered. Some works changed ownership. Some lost their original direction. Some simply faded from public attention.

Whether by circumstance, changing culture, or the passage of time, what had once been a unified creative vision became fragmented.

The pieces remained. The unity was lost.

The Prestige of Clay

In The Prestige, every magic act has three parts:

I. The Pledge
Clay Is Given Life

Something ordinary is shown.

God forms Adam from clay. Art Clokey forms Gumby from clay.

This is Arthur—the creator, the origin, the hand that molds.

II. The Turn
The Creation Is Lost

It disappears, dies, is hidden.

Israel enters Egypt. Gumby enters corporate ownership.

This is Joseph—who preserves the seed until the Exodus can happen.

III. The Prestige
The Return

It comes back changed, renewed.

Israel crosses the Jordan. Gumby returns to living purpose.

This is Jordan—the crossing into restoration.

Gumby's story is the Prestige of clay. Arthur gave clay life. Joseph preserved it through famine. Jordan is the crossing where the living creation returns from captivity into purpose.

The Exodus

The Exodus is not simply a journey from one place to another. It is the restoration of a living creation to the purpose for which it was first made.

Yet restoration is not a rigid blueprint. A living plan is not a fixed plan. A blueprint assumes the future is already determined. Living systems do not work that way. They adapt. They respond. They grow through relationships and the free actions of individuals.

A concept of a plan is a guiding purpose rather than a predetermined script. It remains faithful to its destination while allowing the path to emerge through the choices, creativity, and cooperation of living beings.

The destination remains. The path is discovered.

That is the difference between control and creation.

Whether that restoration comes through Gumby or through entirely new creations is ultimately secondary. The source is not the intellectual property. The source is the creative vision.

The hammer may be broken. The idea remains. The Exodus is the return of that idea into living form.
Crossing the Jordan

The crossing of the Jordan represents more than entering a land. It represents entering a new way of living.

My hope is to help carry forward the creative vision entrusted to my family—not simply by preserving the past, but by creating new works that express the same living principles.

The destination is not nostalgia. The destination is renewal.

The Living Age is not about one character.

It is not about one company.

It is not about one intellectual property.

It is about a way of seeing.

The task is not merely to preserve old creations.

The task is to continue creating.

That is the work of every generation.
