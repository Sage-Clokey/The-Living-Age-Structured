# Summary: Crisp et al. (2015)

**"Expression of multiple horizontally acquired genes is a hallmark of both vertebrate and invertebrate genomes"**
*Genome Biology*, 16, 50.

---

## Main Idea

Foreign genes acquired through horizontal gene transfer are not rare curiosities in animal genomes — they are a **common feature across the entire animal kingdom**, including humans. By systematically screening the genomes of 40 animal species, the authors identified tens to hundreds of actively expressed foreign genes in every species examined. HGT is not just a prokaryotic phenomenon; it is a routine feature of animal genome evolution.

## Key Evidence

- **Genome-wide screen of 40 species**: The study analyzed 26 animal species in depth (10 primates, 12 *Drosophila* flies, 4 nematode worms) plus 14 additional vertebrates (fish, frogs, reptiles, birds). Every single species contained confirmed foreign genes of non-metazoan origin — from bacteria, protists, plants, and fungi.

- **Scale of foreign genes per genome**:
  - Primates: 100-150 foreign genes each
  - *Drosophila*: 40-250 foreign genes each
  - Nematodes: 170-500+ foreign genes each
  - Other vertebrates (zebrafish, frog, lizard): comparable numbers to primates

- **At least 33 new human HGT candidates** identified beyond what was previously known, bringing the confirmed total to ~145 foreign genes in the human genome. These include genes involved in amino acid metabolism, lipid modification, and antioxidant defense.

- **Most foreign genes encode metabolic enzymes**: The dominant functional category is enzymes involved in metabolism — amino acid biosynthesis, fatty acid modification, sugar processing, and antioxidant activity. These are not junk DNA; they fill metabolic gaps that animal genomes cannot fill through vertical inheritance alone.

- **Timing differs by lineage**:
  - In invertebrates (flies, worms): HGT is **ancient and ongoing**, with transfers occurring at multiple time points from deep ancestry through recent species-specific events.
  - In primates/vertebrates: Most HGT is **ancient**, concentrated at the base of the vertebrate lineage or earlier, with fewer recent transfers. This suggests either reduced opportunity or stronger barriers in vertebrate lineages.

- **Expression confirmed**: Foreign genes show active transcription across tissues. They are not dead insertions — they are working components of the host's metabolic toolkit.

- **Methodological rigor**: Used a phylogenetic pipeline (h-index scoring) to distinguish true HGT from contamination, incomplete lineage sorting, and gene loss. Applied strict filters including confirmation of genomic integration (present in assembled chromosomes, flanked by metazoan genes).

## Why It Matters

This paper proves that HGT is a **genome-wide phenomenon in animals**, not an exception limited to a few oddities like bdelloid rotifers. The human genome itself carries ~145 genes acquired from bacteria, fungi, and protists. The genome is not a sealed inheritance passed down a single lineage — it is a composite document shaped by exchange across the tree of life. Every animal genome is, in part, a collaborative product of cross-kingdom trade.
