The Immutable Realities of Existence

Human beings, in every age, have tried to bend reality to their will. Kings decree new orders, priests revise sacred texts, lawmakers craft fresh codes, and scientists propose new theories. Yet beneath all this activity lies an unchanging truth: reality does not conform to opinion.

You cannot alter the laws of physics by rewriting the science books. You cannot change God by retranslating the scriptures. You cannot redefine morality by rewriting the law. These things are woven into the very fabric of existence—unchanging, immovable, not subject to the rise and fall of empires or the whims of majorities.

Physics: The Impersonal Judge

Science is not invented, it is discovered. The laws of nature stand indifferent to human decree. For centuries, textbooks declared the Earth the center of the universe, sanctioned by churches and governments. Yet no decree could stop the Earth from circling the sun. Galileo did not create a new reality—he revealed what had always been there. Human words can mislead or clarify, but the laws themselves remain constant.

God: Beyond Translation

Religious authorities have often sought to reshape God into their own image—revising texts, establishing doctrines, silencing dissent. Yet God is not a manuscript to be edited. Emperors once proclaimed themselves divine, commanding worship and rewriting liturgies, but their cults crumbled with their empires. God cannot be manufactured by decree, nor diminished by misinterpretation. The divine law is inscribed on the human heart, and His reality is glimpsed in shared experience across cultures and ages.

Morality: Deeper than Law

Governments confuse legality with morality, assuming what is written into law must therefore be right. But morality precedes law. A regime can legislate persecution, as in Nazi Germany, and still remain immoral. The Nuremberg Trials made this clear: there exists a higher standard than the law of the state—the natural law of conscience, universal and inescapable. Likewise, the American civil rights movement appealed to eternal principles of justice, not to the convenience of the majority. An unjust law, as Martin Luther King Jr. reminded the world, is no law at all.

Logic: The Science of Morality

Morality, like physics, has its method of discovery. Logic is morality’s science. A conscience may whisper that something feels wrong, but logic tests and refines that intuition. Dialogue among free individuals functions like experiment and replication, exposing contradictions and clarifying truth. In this way, morality emerges not from mob rule but from the wisdom of many consciences reasoning together. It is a spontaneous order, decentralized and resilient, not imposed by force but discovered through freedom.

The True Republic

This is the essence of the true Republic: not majority rule, nor the coercion of empires, but the harmony of conscience and reason freely converging. Democracy without principle can devolve into tyranny of the majority; empires into tyranny of the few. But a Republic of conscience stands upon unshakable ground, because it does not pretend to invent truth. It acknowledges what is higher than itself.

Conclusion

Physics, divinity, and morality form the triune foundation of reality. Physics grounds us in the material world, God anchors us in the transcendent, and morality governs the human sphere where conscience and logic converge. These realities cannot be changed by decree, opinion, or translation. They can only be discovered, lived, and shared.

It is the task of every generation not to rewrite reality, but to uncover it with humility—and to pass on the wisdom of that discovery so that truth may outlast the fragile words of men.
