The Idea: Biology Is the Strongest Case for Liberty That No One Is Making

The most powerful empirical evidence for voluntary order, limited government, and free markets is not in an economics textbook. It is in every living thing on earth — and the Cato Institute has never made this case.

Your body is 37 trillion cells. No president. No congress. No central plan. They coordinate through local signals, voluntary exchange, and distributed knowledge — exactly the way Hayek described a free market. And when one cell overrides that cooperative system — when it centralizes control and demands all resources flow to itself — we have a word for that. Cancer.

This is not a metaphor. This is peer-reviewed molecular biology. Protein-protein interaction networks show no master protein and no central regulator — proteins interact locally and global function emerges. The immune system operates as decentralized defense without a state: autonomous cells detect threats, respond immediately, and coordinate laterally without a bureaucratic approval chain. Cells that are hijacked by a pathogen would rather die than be coerced — apoptosis, programmed cell death, is a cell choosing death over submission to a hijacked program.

At the ecological scale, forests organize through mycorrhizal networks that share resources underground without central allocation. No forester designed it. Monoculture plantations — centrally planned — collapse. Diverse forests — emergent — endure for millennia.

At the genomic scale, James Shapiro’s work on natural genetic engineering shows that genomes don’t evolve through random character-by-character mutation — they restructure themselves by rearranging existing functional modules in new combinations, activated by stress, regulated by the cell itself. The genome operates like a distributed ledger: no central controller, distributed consensus across cells, self-rewriting protocols, and fork events that create new species the way a hard fork creates a new chain. Life doesn’t wait for a central authority to approve a mutation. It engineers its own adaptation from the bottom up.

Hayek’s knowledge problem predicts that the information required to coordinate a complex system never exists in concentrated form. Biology proves this at every scale: no cell holds the full picture, yet the organism functions. Mises’ calculation problem predicts that rational allocation requires prices — and biology uses price-like signals (metabolic shadow prices, hormone gradients, nutrient concentrations) to coordinate resource allocation across trillions of actors. Menger’s spontaneous order predicts that functional organization arises from individual agents responding to local conditions — and this is precisely how ecosystems, immune systems, and gene regulatory networks operate.

The gap is this: Cato has world-class scholars making the economic, legal, and philosophical case for liberty. No one at Cato is making the biological case. No one is showing that the principles Hayek and Mises described are not merely economic — they are the operating principles of life itself. This is the strongest possible ground to stand on, and it is unoccupied.

The audience this unlocks is enormous. There are millions of STEM students, biologists, engineers, and doctors who intuitively understand emergent order from their training but have never connected it to political philosophy. They would never read a policy paper. But they would watch a video called “Cancer Is Central Planning” or “The Open Source Economy of the Cell.” They already think in systems. They just need someone to show them that the system they study every day is the system the liberty movement has been describing in political language for decades.

This is not ideology projected onto nature. This is nature revealing the principle.

The Method

The method follows the structure of how life itself organizes: distribute the work, validate locally, let the pattern emerge.

Content production cycle (monthly): - One long-form essay or research paper connecting a specific biological system to a specific liberty principle (e.g., “The Immune System as Decentralized Defense: What Biology Teaches About Security Without a State”) - Two short-form videos (3-7 minutes) visualizing the biological evidence - One public appearance — campus talk, podcast, conference panel, or Cato event

Research methodology: - Draw from peer-reviewed databases: STRING (protein-protein interactions), RegulonDB (gene regulatory networks), KEGG (metabolic pathways), NCBI (genomic data), single-cell RNA sequencing atlases - Apply Austrian economic frameworks — Hayek’s knowledge problem, Mises’ calculation problem, Kirznerian entrepreneurial discovery — as analytical lenses on biological data - Use bioinformatics tools (UCSC Genome Browser, NCBI Entrez, ESM-3 protein language models, AlphaFold) for original analysis and visualization - Collaborate with Cato scholars to ensure economic and philosophical rigor

Why this is consistent with Cato’s principles: This project does not use biology as a metaphor for liberty. It demonstrates that liberty’s core principles — voluntary exchange, distributed knowledge, spontaneous order, the failure of central planning — are the operating principles of every living system on earth. The method is empirical, the data is peer-reviewed, and the conclusions follow from the evidence. Cato’s mission is to advance individual liberty through scholarship and public engagement. This project does both, from the hardest evidence available — biology itself.

Mission Alignment

Individual liberty. Biology demonstrates that every living system operates through individual agents making autonomous decisions based on local knowledge. No cell is commanded. No protein is conscripted. Voluntary action is the foundation of all biological function. When individual autonomy is overridden — by a virus, a pathogen, or a cancerous cell — the system fails. Liberty is not a political preference. It is how life works.

Limited government. The biological analog of unlimited government is cancer: a cell that overrides the cooperative system, centralizes resource allocation, and demands that all activity serve its growth. Every centralized biological control mechanism — from viral hijacking to autoimmune overreaction — produces disease. Limited, distributed governance is the only model that sustains life at any scale.

Free markets. Biological systems coordinate through price-like signals: metabolic concentrations, hormone gradients, nutrient availability. These signals encode distributed knowledge that no central planner can hold. Hayek’s price system is not a metaphor for biology — biology is the original implementation. Cells trade molecular goods through voluntary exchange. Organisms specialize based on comparative advantage. Ecosystems allocate resources through decentralized negotiation. The free market is four billion years old.

Peace. The immune system is the biological model of defense without empire. It protects the organism without conquering other organisms. It distinguishes self from non-self without aggression toward everything foreign. It maintains tolerance — immunological peace — as its default state, and responds with force only when the boundary is violated. Defense, not offense. Protection, not conquest. This is peace as biology practices it.
