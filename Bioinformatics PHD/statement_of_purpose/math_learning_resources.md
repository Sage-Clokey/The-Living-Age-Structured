# Learning Resources — Mathematics of Spontaneous Order

## 1. Category Theory

### Videos
- [Category Theory for Beginners (A Clear Introduction)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jfkeLKjBlQg) — Feb 2026, explores four levels of abstraction. Best starting point for video.
- [A Sensible Introduction to Category Theory](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yAi3XWCBkDo) — Jun 2022, well-paced walkthrough.
- [A Gentle Introduction to Category Theory](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yP2RjVD-cZ0) — Mar 2025, covers logic, programming, type theory connections.
- [Category Theory For Beginners: Introduction](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=US4Zr1WKD-8) — May 2019, emphasizes category theory as "the perfect language."

### Textbooks
- **Start here → Spivak, D.I. (2014). *Category Theory for the Sciences*.** MIT Press. Written specifically for scientists, example-based rather than proof-based. [Free PDF through MIT OpenCourseWare](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-s996-category-theory-for-scientists-spring-2013/pages/textbook/). This is the one most directly relevant to your work.
- **Lawvere & Schanuel. *Conceptual Mathematics: A First Introduction to Categories*.** Best for absolute zero math background. Lots of drill, accessible to anyone.
- **Agore, A. (2023). *A First Course in Category Theory*.** Springer. Gentle, clear, covers all essentials (categories, functors, natural transformations, limits, adjunctions).
- **Leinster, T. *Basic Category Theory*.** Short, good, but compressed — better as a second read.
- **Mac Lane, S. (1971). *Categories for the Working Mathematician*.** The classic. Dense. Read after Spivak.

### Course
- [MIT OCW 18.S996 — Category Theory for Scientists](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-s996-category-theory-for-scientists-spring-2013/) — Full course with lecture notes, problem sets, and the free Spivak textbook.

## 2. Information Theory

### Videos
- [3Blue1Brown — Solving Wordle Using Information Theory](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v68zYyaEmEA) — Grant Sanderson uses Wordle as an excuse to teach Shannon entropy, bits of information, and optimal information gain. Best visual introduction to the core concepts.

### Textbooks
- **Cover, T.M. & Thomas, J.A. (2006). *Elements of Information Theory*.** Wiley. The standard graduate textbook. Covers entropy, mutual information, KL divergence, channel capacity.
- **MacKay, D.J.C. (2003). *Information Theory, Inference, and Learning Algorithms*.** Cambridge. [Free online](http://www.inference.org.uk/mackay/itila/). Bridges information theory with machine learning and Bayesian inference. More accessible than Cover & Thomas.

### Key Papers
- Shannon, C.E. (1948). "A Mathematical Theory of Communication." *Bell System Technical Journal*. The original — surprisingly readable.
- Tkačik, G. & Bialek, W. (2016). "Information Processing in Living Systems." *Annual Review of Condensed Matter Physics*. Bridges information theory directly to biology.

## 3. Network Theory

### Free Textbook + Course
- **Barabási, A.-L. *Network Science*.** [Free online textbook](https://networksciencebook.com/). The definitive resource. Covers random networks, scale-free networks, small-world property, robustness, communities, spreading phenomena. Includes films and interactive visualizations. Undergraduate and graduate material clearly delineated. **Start here for network theory.**
- [Barabási's Network Science course (archived)](http://barabasilab.neu.edu/courses/phys5116/) — Free archived lectures from Northeastern University.
- [RPI Frontiers of Network Science (CSCI-6250)](https://cs.rpi.edu/~szymansk/fns.23/index.php) — Lecture notes, assignments, solutions using Barabási's textbook.

### Videos
- [Barabási on scale-free networks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RfgjHoVCZwU) — Short intro by Barabási himself on how he discovered scale-free properties.

### Key Papers
- Barabási, A.-L. & Albert, R. (1999). "Emergence of Scaling in Random Networks." *Science*, 286, 509–512.
- Albert, R., Jeong, H., & Barabási, A.-L. (2000). "Error and attack tolerance of complex networks." *Nature*, 406, 378–382. — This is the paper behind your capstone's robustness analysis.
- Watts, D.J. & Strogatz, S.H. (1998). "Collective dynamics of 'small-world' networks." *Nature*, 393, 440–442.

## 4. Statistical Mechanics / Self-Organization / Complexity

### Free Courses
- **[Santa Fe Institute — Introduction to Complexity](https://www.complexityexplorer.org/courses)** — Free, self-paced, open for enrollment now. No prerequisites. Covers dynamics, chaos, fractals, information theory, self-organization, agent-based modeling, and networks. Instructor: Melanie Mitchell. **Best single resource for the complexity side of your work.**
- [Complexity Explorer](https://www.complexityexplorer.org/) — Full platform with additional self-paced courses currently open:
  - Introduction to Dynamical Systems and Chaos
  - Fractals and Scaling
  - Nonlinear Dynamics: Mathematical and Computational Approaches
  - Computation in Complex Systems
  - Origins of Life
  - Tutorials on Information Theory, Game Theory, Machine Learning
- **Mitchell, M. (2009). *Complexity: A Guided Tour*.** Oxford. Won the Phi Beta Kappa Science Book Award. Accessible companion to the course — same ideas, no heavy math.

### Videos
- [Leonard Susskind — Statistical Mechanics Lecture 9](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AT4_S9vQJgc) — Develops the Ising model and phase transitions. Stanford Continuing Studies series. Clear, physical intuition.

### Textbooks
- **Kauffman, S.A. (1993). *The Origins of Order: Self-Organization and Selection in Evolution*.** Oxford. The foundational text for "order for free" — self-organization as a force in evolution independent of selection. Dense but essential. [Available on Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/stuart-a-kauffman-the-origins-of-order-selforganization-and-complexity-in-evolution). Also available as [PDF](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5657eb54e4b022a250fc2de4/t/566fa2ac40667ad8b9b6127c/1450156716552/1993_Kauffman_The+Origins+of+Order.pdf).
- **Kauffman, S.A. (1995). *At Home in the Universe*.** Oxford. The popular version of *Origins of Order* — same ideas, no math. Read this first if *Origins* is too dense.
- **Goldenfeld, N. (1992). *Lectures on Phase Transitions and the Renormalization Group*.** Addison-Wesley. Graduate-level physics but covers the universality and scale invariance that explain why the same patterns appear at biological, economic, and evolutionary scales.
- **Strogatz, S.H. (2015). *Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos*.** Westview Press. The standard undergrad text on dynamical systems, bifurcations, chaos. Beautifully written, many biological examples.

### Blog / Written Resource
- [Phase Transitions & The Ising Model — Ryan Peters](https://www.ryanirl.com/blog/phase-transitions-ising/) — Written from a neuroscience/biology angle. Covers critical brain hypothesis, power-law neuronal avalanches, and why biological systems operate near phase transitions.

## 5. Evolutionary Game Theory

### Video Lectures
- **[Yale Open Course — Lecture 33: Evolutionary Game Theory](https://oyc.yale.edu/ecology-and-evolutionary-biology/eeb-122/lecture-33)** — 45 minutes. Covers Hawk-Dove, Prisoner's Dilemma, ESS, with biological examples. Best single introductory lecture.
- [MathTube — Introduction to Agent-Based Evolutionary Game Theory](https://mathtube.org/lecture/video/introduction-agent-based-evolutionary-game-theory) — Bottom-up approach, applications in biology and economics.
- [MIT OCW — Game Theory (Spring 2024): Evolutionary Game Theory slides](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-126-game-theory-spring-2024/) — Graduate-level lecture slides.

### Textbooks
- **Maynard Smith, J. (1982). *Evolution and the Theory of Games*.** Cambridge. The original — defined ESS, applied game theory to biology.
- **Nowak, M.A. (2006). *Evolutionary Dynamics*.** Harvard. Modern, comprehensive, mathematically rigorous but readable. Covers replicator dynamics, Price equation, spatial games, evolutionary graph theory.
- [Mathematics LibreTexts — Introduction to Evolutionary Game Theory](https://math.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Applied_Mathematics/Agent-Based_Evolutionary_Game_Dynamics_(Izquierdo_Izquierdo_and_Sandholm)/01:_Introduction/1.01:_Introduction_to_evolutionary_game_theory) — Free online textbook chapter.

## 6. Cellular Automata

### Videos
- [Cellular Automata: Life from Simple Rules](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wbPgoZ2d0Nw) — Nov 2022, basic introduction covering Conway's Game of Life rules and emergent behavior.
- [Cellular Automata - Game of Life playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLWZrpIHt9H2J7W-Tyx2AdiqbRwv58BTKq) — Collection of Game of Life videos.

### Interactive Tools
- [Simulations4All — Cellular Automata Explorer](https://simulations4all.com/simulations/cellular-automata-explorer) — Interactive. Wolfram 1D rules (0–255), Conway Game of Life patterns, custom rule editor. Visualize entropy, population dynamics, emergent complexity.

### Textbooks
- **Wolfram, S. (2002). *A New Kind of Science*.** Wolfram Media. Massive (1,200 pages). The argument that simple programs (cellular automata) are sufficient to produce all complexity in nature. Read selectively — Chapters 2–6 cover the core ideas.
- **Ilachinski, A. (2001). *Cellular Automata: A Discrete Universe*.** World Scientific. More formal and mathematical than Wolfram.

## Suggested Learning Path

### Phase 1: Foundations (start here)
1. **[Santa Fe Institute — Introduction to Complexity](https://www.complexityexplorer.org/courses/11)** — free, no prerequisites, covers self-organization, networks, information theory, agent-based modeling in one course
2. **[Barabási — Network Science textbook](https://networksciencebook.com/)** — free, directly extends your capstone work
3. **[3Blue1Brown — Information Theory via Wordle](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v68zYyaEmEA)** — visual intuition for entropy and information

### Phase 2: Deepening
4. **Kauffman — *At Home in the Universe*** — popular version of self-organization thesis, no heavy math
5. **[Yale — Evolutionary Game Theory lecture](https://oyc.yale.edu/ecology-and-evolutionary-biology/eeb-122/lecture-33)** — 45 min, biological game theory
6. **MacKay — *Information Theory, Inference, and Learning Algorithms*** — [free online](http://www.inference.org.uk/mackay/itila/), bridges info theory to computation

### Phase 3: The Mathematical Core
7. **Spivak — *Category Theory for the Sciences*** — [free via MIT OCW](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-s996-category-theory-for-scientists-spring-2013/pages/textbook/), the formal language for proving structural identity
8. **Nowak — *Evolutionary Dynamics*** — formalizes biological markets as game theory
9. **Strogatz — *Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos*** — the math of dynamical systems with biological examples

### Phase 4: Integration
10. **Kauffman — *The Origins of Order*** — the full argument for self-organization in evolution
11. **Cover & Thomas — *Elements of Information Theory*** — graduate reference for entropy, mutual information, KL divergence
12. **Goldenfeld — *Lectures on Phase Transitions and the Renormalization Group*** — universality and scale invariance
