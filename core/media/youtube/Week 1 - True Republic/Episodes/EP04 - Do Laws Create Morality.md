# EP04 — Do Laws Create Morality?

**Series:** True Republic
**Type:** Street / Campus Interview
**Core Question:** "Is something wrong because it's illegal, or illegal because it's wrong?"

---

## The Seed Being Planted

Law and morality are often conflated. But they diverge constantly throughout history —
slavery was legal, harboring Jews in Nazi Germany was illegal.

The Sagent Creed describes "a law written on the human heart that no legislature can repeal
and no tyrant can revise." This episode asks people to find that law in themselves —
and notice that it existed before any government told them about it.

---

## Interview Arc

### Opening Question
"Is something wrong because it's illegal — or illegal because it's wrong?"

### Follow-Up Questions
- "Can you think of something that was legal that was clearly wrong?"
- "Can you think of something illegal that seems morally fine?"
- "Where does your sense of right and wrong come from — law, or something else?"
- "If a law required you to do something you knew was wrong, would you do it?"
- "Does a vote make something moral?"
- "If 51% of people vote to enslave the other 49%, is it moral?"

### The Contradiction That Often Emerges
People will affirm that morality is separate from law — then struggle to explain why
they should obey laws they believe are unjust. The discovery: their conscience was
always prior to the law. The law never created it.

---

## Cold Open Script (Narrator)
> "At the Nuremberg trials, soldiers said they were just following orders.
> The court said that wasn't good enough.
>
> Which means — the court was saying there is a law
> above the law.
>

---

## Closing Narration
The Sagent Creed calls this "the still, deep source every human being carries encoded in
their conscience." Governments don't create moral law — they either reflect it or violate it.
When people discover that their conscience preceded every legal code, they've found something
the jar-shaker cannot shake.
