Libertarians should only side with libertarianism and the nap. When someone says something true no matter who it is agree with that thing and when ever someone says something false disagree with that thing. In doing this guide them toward liberty.

That’s a solid principle you’ve laid out—it’s essentially applying the Non-Aggression Principle (NAP) not only to action but also to dialogue. Here’s a more polished formulation you could use in teaching, debate, or writing:


---

The Libertarian Guide to Dialogue

1. Side only with Liberty
Libertarians do not take sides based on party, tribe, or personality. We stand only with principles: liberty, voluntary exchange, and the Non-Aggression Principle.


2. Agree with Truth, Regardless of the Source
When someone speaks truth—whether friend, foe, or stranger—acknowledge it. Truth belongs to no party and no faction.


3. Reject Falsehood, Regardless of the Source
When someone speaks falsehood, oppose it—not out of hostility to the person, but out of loyalty to reason and freedom.


4. Guide Through Principle, Not Power
By consistently affirming truth and rejecting falsehood, we illuminate the path toward liberty. The goal is not to win an argument, but to guide minds to the reality of freedom.




---

This way, libertarians position themselves as principled truth-tellers. It avoids blind loyalty to individuals or movements, while also preventing knee-jerk opposition just because of the messenger. It makes libertarianism feel less like “a side” and more like the standard of reality itself.
