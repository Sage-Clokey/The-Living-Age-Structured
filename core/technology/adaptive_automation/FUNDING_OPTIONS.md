# The Living Age — Funding Options (Philosophically Clean)

> No government money. No conflicts with the voluntarist / decentralized worldview.
> Non-dilutive where possible. No IP encumbrance.

---

## Tier 1: Strongest Fit — Apply Now or Soon

### Shuttleworth Foundation Fellowship
- **What:** Fellowship for individuals building open tools for social change
- **Amount:** ~1 year salary + expenses + 10:1 match on personal investment (up to 3 years)
- **Equity/IP:** None. No IP claims. Non-dilutive.
- **Deadlines:** May 1 (for September start), November 1 (for March start)
- **Application:** 5-minute video + written application + resume
- **Why it fits:** Open-source compatibility engine + GRN database is exactly their thesis.
  The "grammar of life should not be owned" argument mirrors Shuttleworth's own
  argument about Ubuntu. Philosophy-driven founders are what they fund.
- **Conflict check:** Clean. Private foundation. No government. No IP strings.
- **Link:** https://www.shuttleworthfoundation.org/apply/

### Thiel Fellowship
- **What:** $200K grant for builders under 23 who want to build instead of study
- **Amount:** $200,000 over 2 years
- **Equity/IP:** None. Non-dilutive. No IP claims.
- **Deadline:** Rolling — applications accepted year-round
- **Requirements:** Must be 22 or younger. Must not have completed undergrad degree.
  Must be willing to leave school to build full-time.
- **Why it fits:** Peter Thiel's philosophy — building new things, contrarian bets,
  technology that changes the world rather than incremental improvement — maps directly
  to the living architecture thesis. "Growing houses" is exactly the kind of
  "impossible until it isn't" bet the fellowship was designed for.
- **Conflict check:** Clean. Private foundation. Libertarian-adjacent philosophy
  actually aligns with True Republic framing. No government involvement.
- **Age requirement:** Check eligibility — must be 22 or under at time of application.
- **Link:** https://thielfellowship.org/

### Y Combinator
- **What:** Startup accelerator. 3-month program in San Francisco.
- **Amount:** $500K for 7% equity
- **Equity/IP:** 7% equity — dilutive, but standard and fair for what you get
- **Deadline:** May 4, 2026 (Summer 2026 batch, on-time deadline)
- **Requirements:** 1-minute founder video, written application. Can be pre-product.
  Must commit full-time. Must attend in-person in SF.
- **Why it fits:** YC has funded 121+ biotech startups. The AI + biology + living
  materials intersection is exactly the kind of "looks impossible but isn't"
  bet they make. Working codebase + clear philosophy is a strong signal.
- **Conflict check:** Private equity, not government. You give up 7% but gain
  network, credibility, and momentum. No IP claims beyond standard equity terms.
- **Link:** https://www.ycombinator.com/apply

## Tier 2: Strong Fit — Apply With Working Demo

### Chan Zuckerberg Initiative — Essential Open Source Software for Science (EOSS)
- **What:** Grants for open-source software critical to scientific research
- **Amount:** 2-year grants (Cycle 6 allocated $11.7M across multiple projects,
  co-funded with Kavli Foundation and Wellcome Trust)
- **Equity/IP:** None. Non-dilutive. Open-source requirement aligns with plan.
- **Why it fits:** The compatibility engine and GRN database as open-source
  infrastructure for synthetic biology research is exactly their target.
  They fund maintenance, growth, and community engagement for critical tools.
- **Conflict check:** Private philanthropy. No government. Open-source requirement
  matches the planned strategy (open core, commercial platform on top).
- **Caveat:** Usually requires existing user community. Stronger after Phase 1 demo
  and some iGEM adoption.
- **Link:** https://chanzuckerberg.com/eoss/proposals/

### Alfred P. Sloan Foundation — Open Source in Science
- **What:** Grants for tooling, institutions, and economic models around open
  research software, hardware, and related outputs
- **Amount:** Varies — typically $50K–$500K
- **Equity/IP:** None. Non-dilutive.
- **Why it fits:** They fund the infrastructure and sustainability models around
  open science tools, not just the tools themselves. The Red Hat / open-core model
  for biological design software is exactly the kind of economic model they study.
- **Conflict check:** Clean. Private foundation.
- **Link:** https://sloan.org/programs/digital-technology/open-source-in-science

### IndieBio (SOSV)
- **What:** World's first synthetic biology accelerator. 4-month program with
  BSL-2 wet lab access in SF and NYC.
- **Amount:** $250K initial investment (6-8% equity) + $250K Genesis Consortium
  SAFE ($6M cap) = up to $525K total
- **Equity/IP:** 6-8% equity — dilutive but comes with wet lab access
- **Why it fits:** They are THE synbio accelerator. 300+ companies funded.
  Alumni include MycoWorks ($125M Series C) — a mycelium materials company.
  The living architecture thesis is native to their portfolio.
- **Conflict check:** Private VC. Equity-based. No government. The wet lab access
  is uniquely valuable — hard to get elsewhere at this stage.
- **Caveat:** They may want more bio, less software. The pitch needs to emphasize
  the path to physical organisms, not just design tools.
- **Link:** https://indiebio.co/program/

## Tier 3: Worth Knowing — Apply Strategically

### Omidyar Network
- **What:** Pierre Omidyar (eBay) funds technology that shifts power toward
  individuals and away from institutions
- **Amount:** $100K–$5M (varies by stage and structure)
- **Equity/IP:** Varies — can be grant, equity, or blended
- **Why it fits:** Decentralized, voluntary, living-systems framing is their thesis.
  They fund tools that give individuals capability previously reserved for institutions.
- **Conflict check:** Private. Philosophy-aligned.

### Indie.vc / Calm Fund / Earnest Capital
- **What:** Revenue-based financing. They invest and get paid back as a percentage
  of revenue. No equity, no board, no control.
- **Amount:** $50K–$500K typically
- **Equity/IP:** No equity. Revenue share until repaid (usually capped at 3-5x).
- **Why it fits:** Philosophically aligned with voluntarism — the terms are
  explicitly designed to be non-coercive. No board seat, no control, voluntary terms.
- **Conflict check:** Cleanest possible. Purely commercial relationship.
- **Caveat:** Requires some revenue. Better fit after first paying customers.

### Biotech Angel Investors
- **What:** Individuals who've exited biotech companies and invest their own money
- **Amount:** $25K–$250K per angel
- **Equity/IP:** Equity-based, negotiable terms
- **Where to find them:** AngelList, SynBioBeta network, UCSC alumni network,
  LinkedIn searches for "synthetic biology" + "angel investor"
- **Why it fits:** Fast decisions, no bureaucracy, often bring domain expertise
- **Conflict check:** Private capital. Terms are negotiated directly.

### Climate/Regenerative Tech Funds
- **What:** VC funds specifically investing in biology-based alternatives to
  conventional materials and climate solutions
- **Specific funds:**
  - Congruent Ventures — sustainable systems
  - Breakthrough Energy Ventures (early stage) — Gates-backed, climate tech
  - Regeneration.VC — regenerative economy
  - Lowercarbon Capital — climate technology
- **Amount:** $500K–$5M (seed/pre-seed range)
- **Equity/IP:** Standard VC equity terms
- **Why it fits:** Mycelium composites as carbon-sequestering replacement for
  plastics and concrete is directly in their investment thesis.

## Tier 4: Revenue (The Purest Funding)

### First Paying Customers
- **What:** Charge synthetic biology researchers for the design software
- **Amount:** $50–200/month academic, $500–2,000/month startup
- **Equity/IP:** None. You keep everything.
- **Conflict check:** The cleanest funding possible. No strings. No reporting.
  No philosophy compromises. Just value exchanged for money.
- **How:** Build Phase 1 demo → show to iGEM teams and UCSC researchers →
  convert 10 users at $100/month = $1K MRR → this changes every subsequent
  funding conversation.

### Workshops and Speaking
- **What:** "Design with Life" workshops for architects and biologists
- **Amount:** $200–500/seat, $1K–10K/speaking engagement
- **Equity/IP:** None.
- **Why it fits:** Builds customer pipeline AND revenue simultaneously.
  A sold-out workshop at a university is also a proof-of-market signal
  that investors and foundations understand.

## What to Avoid (Conflict of Interest)

| Source | Conflict |
|--------|----------|
| SBIR/STTR | Government IP license. Reporting burden. Entanglement with agencies that may later regulate living materials. |
| DARPA | Defense funding. Directly conflicts with "stewardship not extraction" philosophy. Creates associations that damage the brand. |
| DOE/USDA grants | Government strings. Same IP issues as SBIR. |
| Corporate partnerships with defense contractors | Obvious. |
| VC funds with government LP money | Less obvious but worth checking — some "private" funds have government pension or sovereign wealth backing. |

## Philosophically Aligned Funding — Liberty, Voluntarism, Open Systems

These are specifically aligned with the True Republic / Spiral Steward worldview:
decentralized systems, voluntary cooperation, individual agency, skepticism of
centralized control. Non-dilutive unless noted.

### Emergent Ventures (Tyler Cowen / Mercatus Center)
- **What:** Fellowship and grant program for "zero to one" ideas that advance
  prosperity, opportunity, liberty, and well-being
- **Amount:** Varies — typically $10K–$100K+
- **Equity/IP:** None. Non-dilutive. No strings.
- **Deadline:** Rolling — guaranteed response within 2-3 weeks
- **Why it fits:** Tyler Cowen funds moonshots at the intersection of technology
  and liberty. "Growing living houses using open-source biodesign tools" is exactly
  the kind of idea he describes as "far from proving effective but could have an
  astronomically positive effect on society." The Mercatus Center is libertarian-adjacent
  (George Mason University). The application is lightweight — Cowen reviews them
  personally. Your Cato University attendance signals you're in the network.
- **Conflict check:** Clean. Private philanthropy via Mercatus Center (GMU).
  No government money. No IP claims. Philosophically aligned with voluntarism.
- **Apply:** https://www.mercatus.org/emergent-ventures
- **PRIORITY: APPLY THIS WEEK. Rolling, fast response, no bureaucracy.**

### O'Shaughnessy Fellowships & Grants
- **What:** $100,000 equity-free fellowship for builders, researchers, and creatives
  "advancing civilization." Also awards up to 20 grants of $10,000.
- **Amount:** $100,000 (fellowship) or $10,000 (grant)
- **Equity/IP:** None. Non-dilutive. Fellows retain full ownership of their work.
  No requirement to form a company.
- **Deadline:** April 30, 2026 (6 weeks away)

- **Why it fits:** Their language is "advancing civilization" — not "maximizing
  shareholder value." The Living Age thesis (biology as the next material of
  civilization) maps directly to their mission. O'Shaughnessy Ventures comes from
  the builder/creator ecosystem, not government or traditional academia.
- **Conflict check:** Clean. Private. No IP claims. No equity. Philosophy-aligned.
- **Apply:** https://forms.osv.llc/fellowships2026
- **PRIORITY: APPLY BY APRIL 30.**

### 1517 Fund — Medici Grant
- **What:** $1,000 micro-grants for students, dropouts, and young builders.
  No strings attached — no IP, no equity, no future claims.
- **Amount:** $1,000
- **Equity/IP:** Explicitly none. "We don't own you, your project, any IP generated,

  Living architecture is sci-fi science. You're their target founder.

  fund's network.
- **Conflict check:** Perfectly clean. Philosophically native.
- **Apply:** https://www.1517fund.com/medici
- **PRIORITY: APPLY TODAY. Takes 10 minutes.**
